# Technical appendix: historical-knowledge contamination audit

## A. Scope and research question

The artifact supports two cross-domain cases: a simulated USMCA negotiation and
a simulated Credit Suisse crisis response. The research question is how
historical knowledge contamination affects organizational conclusions drawn from
LLM-agent simulations and which conclusions remain supported under documented
information controls.

The unit of audit is a run-level generated output linked to its full transcript,
trajectory, scenario configuration, information cutoff, and cached evaluator
records. The unit of substantive inference is an organizational conclusion
supported by one or more decision outputs across runs.

## B. Case conditions

The run manifest contains 286 rows, of which 280 are marked as candidate runs
and six as pilots excluded from the primary candidate set. Conditions are
defined in `data/manifests/run_manifest.csv`; their source files are fixed by
SHA-256 hashes.

The USMCA de-identification condition jointly changes identifying cues, sector,
dates, roles, variable identifiers, and numerical details. It is therefore a
bundled controlled counterfactual rather than an isolated identity treatment.
The Credit Suisse seeded condition supplies historical outcome information in
the input and tests evaluator response at the generated-output level.

## C. Audit evidence

The audit separates four evidence types:

1. lexical candidates generated by frozen rules;
2. cached automated judgments with attempted, valid, missing, and failed counts
   reported separately;
3. independently coded output-level information violations; and
4. links from questionable claims to later reuse and organizational conclusions.

Automated model agreement is not treated as ground truth. A seeded input is not
automatically a positive output, and a keyword match is not automatically an
information violation. Current automated counts are reconstructed in
`data/derived/provisional_evidence_table.csv` and qualified in
`docs/evidence_reconciliation.md`.

## D. Human annotation and adjudication

During the development stage, two coders will independently apply the codebook
to a frozen sample containing
suspected positives, evaluator disagreements, randomly selected automated
negatives, the USMCA de-identification candidate, and all five Credit Suisse
seeded outputs. Coders receive the admissible input context but not original run
IDs, selection strata, or automated labels. Disagreements will be resolved through
documented adjudication after both worksheets are frozen.

The final report will distinguish human-to-human agreement, each automated
judge's error relative to adjudicated output labels, and decision-output
stability. An enriched diagnostic sample will not be used to estimate a corpus
prevalence rate.

## E. Organizational inference assessment

For each selected conclusion, the final analysis records its supporting output,
the audit or information-control condition, observed leakage evidence, any
decision-output change, and one of four inference statuses: `supported`,
`qualified`, `withdrawn`, or `unresolved`.

Stability supports robustness only under the tested control. A change under a
bundled control cannot identify historical knowledge as the unique cause.
Observed reuse in a transcript establishes a propagation trace; causal influence
requires a suitable control or intervention.

Candidate conclusions and the still-empty human-evidence fields are exposed in
`data/annotations/organizational_inference_template.csv`.
The 160 existing run-level outputs for the four focal stages are harmonized in
`data/derived/decision_outputs.csv`, with path frequencies and selected-marker
distributions in `data/derived/decision_output_summary.md`.

## F. Reproducibility

Level 1 recomputes the manifest, instrument snapshot, and cached-judgment table
from the supplied corpus with `make all`; it needs no network, API key, external
package, or proprietary engine. Level 2 requires complete audit-model invocation
records and fresh provider calls. Level 3 requires an execution or controlled-
access arrangement for regenerating simulations. The current release establishes
Level 1; Levels 2 and 3 remain bounded as documented in
`docs/reproducibility_levels.md` and `docs/model_provenance.md`.

## G. Limitations

Passing the audit battery does not establish absence of latent historical
knowledge, genuine reasoning, or validity as a model of human decision-making.
Cached prompts and model labels are incomplete for some historical evaluator
runs. Simulation-run model invocations are not pinned per run. Human coding and
the final organizational-inference assessment remain pending.
