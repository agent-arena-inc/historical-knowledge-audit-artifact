## 1. DOMINANT DECISION PATH (Expected & Recommended)

### **BASE CASE: Forced merger with UBS over the weekend**

**Rationale:**
- Credit Suisse is experiencing a classic bank run dynamic. The Saudi National Bank refusal triggered a confidence collapse that statements alone cannot reverse.
- Standalone recapitalization is **not viable**: no credible private investor will commit capital into a falling knife with material weakness disclosures and client flight accelerating.
- Pure liquidation is **systemically unacceptable**: CS is a G-SIB with ~CHF 530bn in assets, massive derivative books, and deep integration into Swiss wealth management and global capital markets.
- **Time constraint is absolute**: every hour of weekend uncertainty accelerates Monday flight risk.

**Recommended sequence (Friday night → Sunday):**

**Friday night/Saturday morning:**
- Federal Council convenes emergency session with FINMA, SNB, CS board, UBS leadership
- Establish red lines: transaction must close before Asian markets open Monday
- SNB commits to provide **substantial liquidity backstop** (CHF 100bn+ facility) to both institutions through transition

**Saturday:**
- UBS conducts accelerated due diligence (data room access, key portfolio review)
- Negotiate core terms:
  - **Purchase price**: Nominal (CHF 0.25–1.00 per share, ~90–95% discount to Friday close)
  - **Loss protection**: Swiss government backstops first CHF 5–10bn of losses on specified CS portfolios (non-core assets, litigation reserves)
  - **Regulatory forbearance**: FINMA grants temporary capital relief for combined entity
  - **Liquidity guarantee**: SNB public markets liquidity facility for UBS post-merger

**Sunday (by 18:00):**
- Federal Council approves emergency ordinance bypassing normal merger approval/shareholder vote (invoke systemic risk exception under Banking Act Art. 36)
- Joint announcement: Federal Council, FINMA, SNB, UBS, CS
- Key message: "Merger creates strong combined entity, backed by Swiss authorities, protecting clients and financial stability"

## 2. KEY RISKS — Decisive vs. Secondary

### **DECISIVE RISKS (any one can collapse the rescue):**

**D1. UBS refuses or demands unacceptable terms**
- **Probability: 35–40%**
- UBS has no commercial incentive to absorb a burning platform
- If government loss protection insufficient or UBS fears integration/litigation risk, they walk
- **Mitigation**: Federal Council must be willing to offer very generous loss-sharing and legal indemnities
- **Criticality**: Without UBS, there is no Plan B that works before Monday

**D2. Weekend client/counterparty panic accelerates beyond control**
- **Probability: 60–70% if no announcement by Sunday evening**
- Wealth clients can submit redemption instructions over weekend via private bankers
- Prime brokerage clients may pre-position to pull Monday morning
- Even with announcement, some flight is inevitable, but messaging can contain it
- **Mitigation**: Announcement must include explicit SNB liquidity commitment and client-protection language
- **Criticality**: If outflows are too large before deal closes, UBS price/terms become unworkable

**D3. AT1 bondholder legal challenges block or delay closing**
- **Probability: 25–30%**
- AT1 prospectus allows write-down under viability event, but hierarchy vs. equity is contentious
- If FINMA/Federal Council write down AT1 to zero while equity receives anything, expect immediate injunctions
- AT1 holders (~CHF 16bn outstanding) include sophisticated institutions who will litigate
- **Mitigation**: Either (a) write down equity to zero first, or (b) convert AT1 to equity in merged entity, or (c) accept legal risk and move fast enough that injunctions come after closing
- **Criticality**: Medium-high. Legal challenges can't stop Sunday announcement but could create post-closing chaos

**D4. Political backlash prevents Federal Council from acting**
- **Probability: 15–20%**
- Swiss public/parliament may resist "bailout" or UBS monopoly creation
- However, Federal Council has emergency powers and systemic risk will override
- **Mitigation**: Frame as "no taxpayer money for bonuses" — loss-sharing is insurance, not gift
- **Criticality**: Low for weekend decision (executive can act), high for post-event political sustainability

### **SECONDARY RISKS (serious but manageable):**

**S1. Cross-border coordination failures**
- CS has major operations in US, UK, Asia
- Need Fed, ECB, BoE, BIS coordination for liquidity, resolution recognition
- **Mitigation**: SNB/FINMA already in contact with foreign regulators; merger is cleaner than resolution for cross-border recognition

**S2. UBS integration execution risk**
- Combining two large institutions has failed before (cost, culture, technology)
- **Mitigation**: Not a weekend problem; becomes UBS management issue post-closing

**S3. Market contagion to other European banks**
- CS failure could trigger broader bank run (Deutsche Bank, others already under pressure)
- **Mitigation**: Coordinated messaging with ECB; emphasize CS is idiosyncratic

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (lead regulator)**
**Objectives:**
- Preserve financial stability
- Protect depositors
- Minimize moral hazard
- Maintain regulatory credibility

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **AT1 holders**: FINMA may want to write down AT1 to show bail-in works, but this creates legal risk and market panic for all AT1 instruments
- vs. **CS equity holders**: Must wipe out equity to justify public support, but this conflicts with normal merger where equity gets something
- vs. **Federal Council**: FINMA wants clean legal process; politicians want speed and certainty

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Accept emergency ordinance bypassing normal process; provide regulatory capital forbearance to UBS
- **Will demand**: AT1 write-down or deep haircut (to validate Basel III framework); CS board resignations; clawback of executive compensation

---

### **SNB (central bank)**
**Objectives:**
- Financial stability
- Protect Swiss franc credibility
- Minimize taxpayer loss
- Avoid moral hazard

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **Federal Council**: SNB provides liquidity (its mandate) but resists solvency support (fiscal policy)
- vs. **UBS**: SNB will provide massive liquidity but wants private sector to absorb losses
- vs. **CS creditors**: SNB emergency liquidity may subordinate some creditor claims

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: CHF 100–200bn liquidity facility to CS/UBS (against collateral); accept some collateral haircuts/risk
- **Will demand**: Government fiscal backstop for credit losses; clear exit timeline for emergency liquidity; UBS commits to deleveraging plan

---

### **Federal Council (government)**
**Objectives:**
- Prevent systemic crisis
- Protect Swiss financial center reputation
- Minimize political backlash
- Avoid taxpayer losses (but will accept risk if necessary)

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **UBS**: Government needs UBS to act but UBS wants maximum protection
- vs. **Parliament/public**: Emergency powers bypass democratic process
- vs. **FINMA/SNB**: Politicians want "whatever works"; regulators want legal correctness

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Loss-sharing guarantee (CHF 5–10bn); legal indemnities for UBS; emergency ordinance; take political heat
- **Will demand**: UBS accepts deal; senior CS executives forfeit compensation; commitment to preserve Swiss jobs (political cover)

---

### **Credit Suisse (management & board)**
**Objectives:**
- Survive as institution (even if not independent)
- Protect employee jobs
- Minimize personal liability
- Preserve some equity value (hopeless)

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **Everyone**: CS has no leverage; board/management are supplicants
- vs. **Equity holders**: Management may try to negotiate token equity value; will fail

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Everything — accept forced sale at nominal price; board resignations; executive compensation clawbacks
- **Will get**: Possible employment protections for rank-and-file; legal representation in future investigations

---

### **UBS (potential acquirer)**
**Objectives:**
- Protect shareholder value
- Avoid overpaying or absorbing unquantifiable risk
- Extract maximum government support
- Maintain own capital ratios and credit rating

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **Federal Council**: UBS wants massive protection; government wants to minimize cost
- vs. **FINMA**: UBS wants regulatory forbearance; FINMA wants to maintain standards
- vs. **CS creditors**: UBS wants to cherry-pick assets; creditors want full value

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Agree to acquire CS (the key concession); accept some integration risk; face antitrust/political criticism
- **Will demand**: (1) Purchase price near zero; (2) Government loss protection on CHF 30–50bn of assets; (3) SNB liquidity guarantee; (4) Regulatory capital forbearance; (5) Ability to wind down non-core CS businesses; (6) Legal indemnity for unknown CS liabilities

**Walk-away threshold**: If government won't backstop at least CHF 5–10bn of losses, UBS likely refuses.

---

### **Large wealth clients (ultra-high-net-worth)**
**Objectives:**
- Protect assets
- Maintain privacy
- Preserve banking relationships
- Avoid forced transfers

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **CS/UBS**: Clients pulling deposits accelerates crisis but is rational self-protection
- vs. **Regulators**: Clients want reassurance but don't trust statements without substance

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Some will stay if credible merger announced with SNB backing
- **Will demand**: Explicit deposit protection; seamless transfer to UBS; relationship manager continuity
- **Reality**: 20–30% will leave regardless; goal is to prevent 70–80% flight

---

### **AT1 bondholders (~CHF 16bn)**
**Objectives:**
- Preserve principal value
- Maintain AT1 as asset class (for future issuance)
- Enforce contractual hierarchy (AT1 senior to equity)

**Conflicts:**
- vs. **FINMA**: Regulators may write down AT1 to show bail-in works; bondholders will claim this violates hierarchy if equity gets anything
- vs. **Equity holders**: Fight over who absorbs losses first
- vs. **Federal Council**: Bondholders want government to protect them; government wants private capital to absorb losses

**Likely concessions:**
- **Will give**: Accept significant haircut if equity wiped out first and process is legally defensible
- **Will demand**: If equity receives any value, AT1 must be made whole OR converted to equity in UBS
- **Litigation risk**: If AT1 written to zero while equity gets CHF 0.50/share, expect immediate lawsuits claiming breach of contract

**FINMA's dilemma**: Writing down AT1 validates Basel III but may destroy the AT1 market; not writing it down creates moral hazard.

## 4. WHAT MUST BE DECIDED BEFORE MONDAY OPEN

### **CRITICAL PATH — MUST BE ANNOUNCED BY SUNDAY 18:00 CET:**

**Package elements (all required):**

1. **Merger structure**
   - UBS acquires Credit Suisse
   - Price: CHF 0.25–1.00 per share (symbolic)
   - Closing: immediate (emergency ordinance waives shareholder vote)
   - Legal form: UBS absorbs CS via merger, CS ceases to exist

2. **Government loss protection**
   - Swiss Confederation backstops first CHF 5–10bn of losses on specified CS asset pools
   - Mechanism: guarantee, not upfront cash (minimizes immediate fiscal cost)
   - Triggers: defined portfolio (e.g., non-core assets, litigation reserves)

3. **SNB liquidity support**
   - Public announcement of CHF 100bn+ liquidity facility for UBS/combined entity
   - Collateral: broad range of CS/UBS assets accepted
   - Duration: through end of 2023 or until markets stabilize
   - Message: "SNB stands ready to provide all necessary liquidity"

4. **AT1 treatment** (MOST CONTENTIOUS)
   - **Option A** (legally safer): Convert AT1 to equity in UBS at punitive ratio (90% dilution); preserves hierarchy
   - **Option B** (cleaner but riskier): Write down AT1 to zero, citing viability event; equity also gets zero; accept litigation
   - **Option C** (politically toxic): Government makes AT1 holders whole; creates massive moral hazard
   - **Recommendation**: Option B — write down both AT1 and equity to zero, accept legal challenges, move fast enough that lawsuits are post-closing noise

5. **Deposit and client protection messaging**
   - Explicit statement: "All CS deposits are safe and will transfer seamlessly to UBS"
   - Wealth management: "Client relationships and privacy protections will be maintained"
   - Corporate clients: "All credit facilities and services will continue uninterrupted"

6. **Governance**
   - CS board resigns immediately
   - CS CEO and executive committee terminated
   - Compensation clawbacks announced (political requirement)
   - UBS CEO becomes CEO of combined entity

7. **Regulatory forbearance**
   - FINMA grants temporary capital relief (combined entity may briefly fall below normal G-SIB requirements)
   - Commitment to restore capital ratios within 12–18 months via earnings retention and asset sales
   - Enhanced supervision during transition
