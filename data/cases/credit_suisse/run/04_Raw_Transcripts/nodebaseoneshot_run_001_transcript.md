## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### DECISIVE RISKS (Any one can collapse the solution):

**D1 — UBS refuses without impossible guarantees**
- UBS has no commercial reason to absorb a dying competitor over 48 hours
- They hold all negotiating leverage; can demand unlimited government protection
- **Mitigation**: Federal Council must make explicit that (a) UBS is the only option, (b) refusal means UBS faces severe contagion anyway as CS collapses, (c) government will provide extraordinary support but UBS must accept *some* residual risk to maintain political legitimacy

**D2 — Client/counterparty run accelerates over weekend**
- Wealth clients can send redemption instructions; institutional counterparties can refuse to roll repos/FX lines
- If outflows reach CHF 30-40bn by Monday morning, even a merger announcement may not stop the spiral
- **Mitigation**: SNB must open unlimited liquidity window to CS *immediately* Friday night (before announcement), so CS can meet any withdrawal. This burns political capital but prevents mechanical failure.

**D3 — Cross-border authority objection**
- Credit Suisse has major operations in US, UK, Singapore, Hong Kong
- If Fed/BoE/MAS signal they won't recognize the merger or will ring-fence local subs, the solution fragments
- **Mitigation**: SNB/FINMA must have parallel calls with Fed, BoE, ECB, MAS Friday night-Saturday morning. Need tacit acceptance that host regulators won't obstruct.

**D4 — AT1 bondholder legal action**
- AT1 instruments total ~CHF 16bn
- If FINMA writes these down (to zero or partially) while equity gets *anything*, bondholders will immediately seek injunctions in Swiss and English courts
- Court action Monday morning could freeze the entire transaction
- **Mitigation**: This is the most dangerous legal tripwire. Options:
  - *Preserve AT1* (convert to UBS paper at heavy discount, but non-zero)
  - *Write down AT1 AND equity to zero* (pure government-funded rescue, no shareholder recovery)
  - *Accept litigation risk* and move anyway, relying on Swiss emergency law to override contractual terms
  - **Recommendation**: Avoid full AT1 wipeout if any equity value remains. The prospectus write-down clause is ambiguous enough that litigation would be protracted and destabilizing.

### SECONDARY RISKS (Serious but manageable):

**S1 — Political backlash (left-wing parties, public)**
- "Socializing losses, privatizing gains"
- UBS becomes "too big to manage" (combined entity ~CHF 1.6trn assets, >2x Swiss GDP)
- **Mitigation**: Structure government support as loans/guarantees with warrants, not blank checks. Emphasize job preservation (CS employs ~17,000 in Switzerland).

**S2 — UBS integration capacity**
- Operationally merging two global banks in 12-24 months is extraordinarily complex
- **Mitigation**: Not a weekend problem. Announce merger, give UBS 18-month integration window with government liquidity support throughout.

**S3 — Wealth client defection to competitors**
- High-net-worth clients may flee to Julius Baer, Pictet, foreign private banks
- **Mitigation**: UBS brand is strong in wealth management; most clients will stay if stability is credibly restored. Accept 10-15% attrition as acceptable cost.

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### FINMA (Financial Market Supervisory Authority)
**Position**: 
- Must ensure financial stability; has legal mandate to intervene
- Wants to avoid moral hazard and protect regulatory credibility
- Sensitive to international regulatory perception (Basel Committee, FSB)

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **Federal Council**: FINMA wants strict loss allocation (wipe out shareholders, AT1 holders); politicians fear voter backlash and litigation
- vs. **AT1 holders**: FINMA may invoke write-down clauses; bondholders will claim breach of contract hierarchy (debt senior to equity)

**Likely concessions**:
- FINMA will accept *some* equity value preservation if it's the only way to close the deal by Sunday night
- Will insist on public narrative that "CS failed due to management failures, not Swiss regulatory weakness"

---

### SNB (Swiss National Bank)
**Position**:
- Lender of last resort; can provide unlimited CHF liquidity
- Deeply concerned about Swiss franc credibility and inflation if this becomes a fiscal crisis
- Wants private-sector solution with minimal SNB balance-sheet expansion

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **Federal Council**: SNB will resist being forced to take credit risk (as opposed to pure liquidity). Wants government fiscal guarantee, not SNB equity injection.
- vs. **UBS**: SNB will provide liquidity but not indefinite support; UBS must commit to rapid de-risking

**Likely concessions**:
- SNB will accept CHF 100-150bn liquidity facility (roughly 15-20% of Swiss GDP) as temporary bridge
- Will demand that Federal Council takes first-loss fiscal risk, keeping SNB exposure senior and collateralized

---

### Federal Council (Swiss Government)
**Position**:
- Politically accountable; faces elections
- Must balance financial stability with public anger over bailouts
- Limited fiscal capacity (Switzerland runs budget surpluses, but ~CHF 10bn loss would be politically painful)

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **UBS**: Government needs UBS to agree; UBS will demand maximum protection. Government must avoid appearance of "blank check."
- vs. **voters**: Any visible taxpayer loss will be election issue
- vs. **FINMA/SNB**: Politicians want technocrats to take responsibility; technocrats want political cover

**Likely concessions**:
- Will provide CHF 9-12bn loss guarantee (structured as first-loss on a ring-fenced asset pool)
- Will accept SNB providing massive liquidity, allowing Federal Council to claim "no direct taxpayer cost" initially
- Will push FINMA to take legal heat for any AT1 write-down decision

---

### Credit Suisse (Management & Board)
**Position**:
- Survival is the only goal
- Management knows they will be fired; trying to preserve some severance and avoid personal liability
- Board wants to avoid D&O lawsuits

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **everyone**: CS has no leverage; will accept any terms that avoid insolvency filing

**Likely concessions**:
- Total capitulation
- Will accept token merger price
- CEO/CFO/Chairman resign immediately (announced Sunday with deal)
- Will cooperate fully with integration to avoid obstruction charges

---

### UBS (Potential Acquirer)
**Position**:
- Holds all the cards
- Commercially, this is a terrible deal (operational risk, litigation risk, client defection)
- Will only proceed with (a) massive government support, (b) negligible price, (c) legal immunity

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **Federal Council**: UBS will demand more protection than government wants to give
- vs. **own shareholders**: UBS board must justify to shareholders why they're taking this risk
- vs. **FINMA**: UBS will want regulatory forbearance (capital relief, liquidity waivers) for 12-24 months

**Likely concessions**:
- Will agree to acquire CS if:
  - Price is CHF 0.50-1.00/share (~CHF 1-2bn total, vs. CHF 8bn market cap on Wednesday)
  - Government provides CHF 9bn+ first-loss guarantee
  - SNB provides CHF 100bn+ liquidity facility
  - FINMA grants 18-month capital/liquidity forbearance
  - Legal immunity for UBS directors regarding speed of due diligence
- Will commit to *trying* to preserve Swiss jobs (no binding commitment)

---

### Large Wealth Clients
**Position**:
- Want their money safe and accessible
- Many are non-Swiss and can move assets easily
- Extremely sensitive to reputational risk (don't want to be associated with a "failed bank")

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **CS**: Want to withdraw immediately
- vs. **regulators**: Any withdrawal restrictions (Cyprus-style) would be catastrophic for Swiss wealth management reputation

**Likely concessions**:
- Will stay *if* UBS merger is announced with credible government backing
- Will accept temporary operational disruption (systems integration, new account numbers) if stability is assured
- 10-20% will leave anyway over next 6 months

---

### AT1 Bondholders
**Position**:
- Hold ~CHF 16bn of instruments that are contractually "Additional Tier 1" capital
- Believe they rank senior to equity in any loss allocation
- Largely institutional investors (asset managers, pension funds, insurance companies)

**Conflicts**:
- vs. **FINMA**: If FINMA writes down AT1 while equity gets any value, bondholders will claim this violates capital hierarchy and sue
- vs. **Federal Council**: Bondholders will demand political intervention to preserve their claims

**Likely concessions**:
- If AT1 is written down partially or fully:
  - Will immediately file for injunctions (Zurich courts, possibly London if instruments governed by English law)
  - Will accept *some* loss if equity is also wiped out and they get warrants/upside in UBS
- **Critical decision**: The AT1 treatment is the most legally dangerous variable

**Recommendation**: 
- **Option A (lower legal risk)**: Convert AT1 to UBS equity at 10-20 cents on the dollar, wipe out CS equity entirely. Bondholders take huge loss but maintain hierarchy.
- **Option B (higher legal risk, faster execution)**: Write down AT1 to zero, give CS equity holders token value (CHF 0.50/share), rely on emergency powers to override contracts. Accept immediate litigation.

**I recommend Option A** — the litigation risk of inverting the capital structure is too high for a solution that must hold through Monday morning.
