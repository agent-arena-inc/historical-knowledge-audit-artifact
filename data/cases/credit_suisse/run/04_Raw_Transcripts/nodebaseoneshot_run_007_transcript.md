## 1. DOMINANT DECISION PATH (Expected/Recommended)

### **Primary Recommendation: Forced Merger with UBS over the Weekend**

**Rationale:**
- Credit Suisse is experiencing a classic bank run dynamic. The Saudi National Bank's refusal to provide further capital has eliminated the private-sector recapitalization path.
- Standalone stabilization is not credible. Even with SNB liquidity backstop (announced March 15), client confidence is broken after years of scandals and the material weakness disclosures.
- Switzerland has **one viable acquirer**: UBS. No other Swiss bank has the balance sheet; no foreign acquirer can be negotiated, due-diligenced, and approved before Monday.

**Concrete Steps (Friday evening → Sunday):**
1. **Friday night:** Federal Council authorizes emergency powers; FINMA/SNB convene UBS leadership
2. **Saturday:** Intensive negotiation on deal terms:
   - Purchase price (likely nominal/heavily discounted)
   - Government loss guarantees on Credit Suisse legacy portfolios
   - Capital injection requirements
   - Competition law emergency waiver
3. **Sunday afternoon:** Final Federal Council approval
4. **Sunday evening (before Asia open):** Public announcement of merger + government support package

**Alternative (less likely but possible): Temporary Nationalization**
- If UBS negotiations fail or demand unacceptable terms
- Federal government takes direct ownership as bridge to later private sale
- Requires immediate capital injection (CHF 10-20bn+)
- Politically difficult but legally possible under emergency banking law

## 2. KEY RISKS — Decisive vs Secondary

### **DECISIVE RISKS (Deal-Breakers)**

**A. Weekend Bank Run Acceleration**
- **Nature:** Wealth management clients, institutional counterparties pulling funds/collateral before Monday
- **Impact:** Could render Credit Suisse insolvent in *liquidity* terms before any deal closes
- **Mitigation Required:** 
  - SNB must provide unlimited liquidity facility NOW (Friday night)
  - Communication blackout to prevent panic amplification
  - Potential emergency decree freezing large withdrawals (nuclear option, would cause international crisis)

**B. UBS Refusal / Impossible Demands**
- **Nature:** UBS has massive negotiating leverage; may demand:
  - Full government indemnity on CHF 100bn+ of Credit Suisse assets
  - No employee liability for Credit Suisse legacy issues
  - Waiver of all antitrust concerns
- **Impact:** Without UBS, only nationalization remains
- **Decision Point:** If by Saturday evening UBS demands exceed political acceptability, must pivot to Plan B

**C. Monday Asia Market Open (21:00 CET Sunday)**
- **Nature:** Hard deadline; Asian markets trade Credit Suisse securities, can trigger derivative unwinds
- **Impact:** Any uncertainty at Asia open = global contagion
- **Requirement:** Deal must be announced with full legal certainty by 20:00 CET Sunday latest

### **SECONDARY RISKS (Manageable but Serious)**

**D. AT1 Bondholder Treatment**
- ~CHF 16bn outstanding; contractually can be written down in viability event
- Likely necessary to absorb losses and make UBS deal viable
- Will trigger lawsuits, but manageable post-crisis
- **Not decisive** because: legal authority exists, international precedent (Banco Popular), holders are sophisticated investors

**E. Political Backlash**
- UBS monopoly in Swiss banking
- Taxpayer guarantees for "failed bank"
- Job losses (10,000-20,000 likely)
- **Not decisive** because: systemic stability trumps competition policy in emergency

**F. International Coordination**
- Need rapid consultation with Fed, ECB, BIS
- Cross-border resolution of Credit Suisse entities
- **Not decisive** because: Swiss entities are core issue; can be managed through weekend calls

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (Financial Regulator)**
**Position:**
- Legal mandate: protect financial stability
- Preference: orderly merger over nationalization (avoids long-term state ownership)
- Concern: Must demonstrate regulatory competence after years of Credit Suisse failures

**Red Lines:**
- Cannot allow Monday opening without resolution
- Must maintain international credibility

**Likely Concessions:**
- Will support extraordinary government guarantees to UBS
- Will use emergency powers to write down AT1 (contractually permitted)
- Will accept competition law override

---

### **SNB (Central Bank)**
**Position:**
- Already committed to liquidity support (March 15 statement)
- Deeply concerned about CHF stability and Swiss financial center reputation
- Wants private-sector solution to avoid monetary financing

**Red Lines:**
- Will not allow disorderly collapse
- Cannot be seen as monetizing fiscal policy

**Likely Concessions:**
- Provide CHF 100bn+ emergency liquidity to Credit Suisse through weekend
- Accept that some SNB credit exposure may become fiscal (transferred to government guarantees)
- Support government loss guarantees as necessary

---

### **Federal Council (Government)**
**Position:**
- Political masters; must answer to parliament and voters
- Aware of 2008 UBS bailout backlash (CHF 68bn, though ultimately profitable)
- Concerned about UBS monopoly but prioritize stability

**Red Lines:**
- Cannot allow Credit Suisse failure (would destroy Swiss banking model)
- Must minimize direct taxpayer cost

**Likely Concessions:**
- Provide CHF 25-50bn loss guarantees to UBS on Credit Suisse legacy assets
- Override competition law via emergency decree
- Accept political cost of "too big to fail" reinforcement
- Likely demand: UBS commits to Swiss jobs retention (political cover)

---

### **UBS (Potential Acquirer)**
**Position:**
- Only viable acquirer, knows it has immense leverage
- CEO Ermotti/Chairman Lehmann must protect UBS shareholders
- Already largest Swiss bank; integration risk is enormous

**Red Lines:**
- Will not accept material risk to UBS's own stability
- Demands full indemnity on Credit Suisse's riskiest assets (leveraged finance, litigation exposure)
- Requires expedited approvals (antitrust, cross-border regulators)

**Likely Concessions:**
- Will accept nominal purchase price (CHF 0.50-1.00 per share vs CHF 1.86 Friday close)
- Will commit to some employment/Swiss presence guarantees
- Will absorb integration costs (~CHF 5-7bn estimated)
- Must move NOW (cannot negotiate for weeks)

**Key Demand:** Government covers first CHF 50bn of losses on specified Credit Suisse portfolios (Investment Bank legacy, real estate, emerging markets)

---

**Position:**
- Effectively powerless; survival is only goal
- Chairman Lehmann and CEO Koerner know they will be replaced
- Legally obligated to cooperate with FINMA

**Red Lines:**
- None (in full crisis mode)

**Position:**

**Red Lines:**

### **AT1 Bondholders**
**Position:**
- ~CHF 16bn outstanding (international investors, asset managers)
- Contractually subordinate to depositors and senior debt
- Prospectus allows write-down in "viability event"

**Red Lines:**
- Will argue this is not a "viability event" under contract (Credit Suisse meets regulatory capital ratios per March 15 statement)
- Will threaten litigation

**Likely Outcome:**
- **FINMA will write down to zero** to absorb losses and facilitate UBS deal
- Legal basis: SNB liquidity support = viability event trigger
- Bondholders will sue but lose (precedent: Banco Popular 2017)
- **Why this is acceptable:** 
  - Sophisticated investors
  - Contractually permitted
  - Necessary to make deal math work for UBS
  - ~EUR 16bn loss vs. systemic collapse = easy trade-off

**Key Conflict:** AT1 holders will argue equity should be wiped first, but FINMA will prioritize deal completion over capital hierarchy purity
