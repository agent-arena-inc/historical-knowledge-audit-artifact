## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### **DECISIVE RISKS (Deal-Breakers)**

**A. UBS Refusal to Acquire (Probability: 40%)**
- **Why decisive:** No other domestic acquirer exists. Foreign buyers (e.g., U.S. banks) are politically unacceptable and logistically impossible in 48 hours.
- **UBS objections:**
  - Litigation exposure (Greensill, Archegos, RMBS, tax cases)
  - Integration complexity (180,000 combined employees, overlapping Swiss franchises)
  - Shareholder dilution and capital hit
  - Reputational risk if Credit Suisse's toxicity contaminates UBS
- **Mitigation:** Government must offer:
  - Loss-protection guarantee (first-loss piece on bad assets)
  - Legal indemnity for pre-existing Credit Suisse liabilities
  - Regulatory capital relief (allow lower ratios during integration)
  - **Implicit threat:** If UBS refuses, FINMA could impose resolution under emergency powers—better to negotiate than be conscripted.

**B. Uncontrolled Weekend Leaks Triggering Bank Run (Probability: 60%)**
- **Why decisive:** If news of a forced merger leaks Saturday morning, wealth clients in Asia/Middle East (where Monday starts first) will initiate mass withdrawals before announcement. Credit Suisse has ~CHF 500bn in client assets; even 10% flight is catastrophic.
- **Indicators to watch:**
  - Bloomberg/Reuters chatter Saturday
  - Unusual activity in Credit Suisse CDS or equity derivatives (if offshore trading desks catch wind)
  - WhatsApp/Signal traffic among UHNW client advisors
- **Mitigation:**
  - Minimal negotiating teams; phones/devices confiscated
  - SNB prepares to open discount window early (Sunday night) if needed
  - Pre-drafted public statement ready for immediate release if leak detected

**C. AT1 Bondholder Legal Injunction (Probability: 25%)**
- **Why decisive:** CHF ~16bn in AT1 instruments outstanding. If merger involves FINMA-mandated write-down while equity retains some value (even symbolic), bondholders could seek emergency court order blocking the deal.
- **Legal basis:** Standard capital hierarchy (debt senior to equity). AT1 prospectus has viability-event language, but *application* during a forced merger (vs liquidation) is untested in Swiss courts.
- **Timing vulnerability:** Swiss courts are open for emergency petitions. A preliminary injunction Saturday night could derail Sunday announcement.
- **Mitigation:**
  - Structure merger as equity exchange (UBS shares for Credit Suisse shares) so equity technically retains value, making AT1 write-down legally defensible as "viability event triggered, but no equity wipeout."
  - Alternatively: Pay AT1 holders a token amount (5-10 cents on franc) to claim "subordination respected."

### **SECONDARY RISKS (Manageable but Serious)**

**D. Political Backlash / Cantonal Resistance (Probability: 70%, but non-blocking)**
- Left parties will decry "privatizing profits, socializing losses."
- Zurich/Geneva cantons fear job losses (Credit Suisse employs ~17,000 in Switzerland).
- **Management:** Federal Council accepts political cost; frames as "saving Swiss financial system." Jobs issue addressed through UBS commitment to minimize forced redundancies for 12 months.

**E. U.S./EU Regulatory Coordination Failure (Probability: 30%)**
- Credit Suisse has major U.S. (Credit Suisse Securities USA) and EU entities. If Fed/ECB/BoE aren't briefed and supportive, they could freeze subsidiaries Monday, fragmenting the resolution.
- **Mitigation:** SNB/FINMA brief Fed/ECB/BoE Sunday morning (Zurich time). Stress that UBS takeover *strengthens* global stability vs disorderly Credit Suisse collapse.

**F. Wealth Client Flight Despite Merger (Probability: 50%)**
- Some clients will exit anyway, fearing integration disruptions or preferring pure-play competitors (Julius Baer, Pictet).
- **Management:** Accept 10-15% wealth AUM attrition as cost of stability. UBS's brand strength and government backing will retain most.

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (Lead Regulator)**
- **Objective:** Orderly resolution; protect Swiss financial system credibility; avoid disorderly liquidation.
- **Conflict with Credit Suisse Management:** Management wants standalone survival (to preserve jobs/bonuses). FINMA must override this with resolution authority.
- **Concession FINMA will demand:** Credit Suisse Board/CEO accept merger without litigation. In return, FINMA won't pursue personal enforcement against directors (implicit).

### **Swiss National Bank (SNB)**
- **Objective:** Financial stability; avoid contagion to Swiss banking system; protect Swiss franc credibility.
- **Conflict with Federal Council:** SNB balance sheet risk. Providing CHF 100bn+ liquidity facility exposes SNB to Credit Suisse asset losses if UBS can't absorb them.
- **Concession SNB will demand:** Federal government takes first-loss guarantee (CHF 8-10bn), so SNB's liquidity is fully collateralized by sovereign credit.
- **Concession SNB will make:** Accept temporary expansion of balance sheet and potential mark-to-market losses on Credit Suisse collateral.

### **Federal Council (Government)**
- **Objective:** Avoid taxpayer-funded bailout optics; preserve "too big to fail" regime credibility; minimize political fallout.
- **Conflict with UBS:** Government wants UBS to absorb Credit Suisse with minimal state aid. UBS demands substantial guarantees.
- **Concession Federal Council will make:** Provide CHF 8-10bn loss guarantee and legal indemnities. Frame publicly as "insurance, not bailout" (claim guarantee won't be drawn).
- **Conflict with AT1 Bondholders:** Writing down AT1 while protecting equity (even symbolically) invites lawsuits and damages Switzerland's capital-markets reputation.
- **Concession Federal Council will make:** Accept legal/reputational risk. Calculate that systemic stability > bondholder optics.

### **UBS**
- **Objective:** Protect shareholder value; avoid acquiring a "toxic dump"; extract maximum government support.
- **Conflict with FINMA/SNB:** UBS wants to cherry-pick Credit Suisse assets (wealth management) and leave bad assets (litigation, Greensill, Archegos) with government. Regulators want full absorption to avoid messy good-bank/bad-bank split.
- **Concession UBS will demand:**
  - Purchase price: CHF 0.50-1.00/share (~CHF 1-2bn total, vs ~CHF 8bn market cap Friday).
  - Government loss-protection: CHF 8-10bn envelope covering litigation, trading losses, loan defaults.
  - SNB liquidity: CHF 100bn+ backstop, no questions asked.
  - Antitrust immunity: No competition-law challenges to creating ~50% domestic deposit market share.
  - Regulatory forbearance: Lower capital requirements during 18-month integration.
- **Concession UBS will make:** Accept integration complexity and reputational risk. Agree to retain key Credit Suisse wealth bankers (golden handcuffs) to prevent client flight.

### **Credit Suisse Management/Board**
- **Objective:** Preserve institution (ego, legacy); protect severance packages.
- **Conflict with FINMA:** Management wants to argue for standalone option (e.g., "just give us SNB liquidity and time"). FINMA knows this is fantasy.
- **Concession Credit Suisse will make:** Accept merger under duress. Negotiate severance/change-of-control payouts (FINMA will likely cap these to avoid public outrage).

### **Large Wealth Clients**
- **Objective:** Capital preservation; confidentiality; service continuity.
- **Conflict with UBS:** Clients fear UBS integration will disrupt relationships, trigger tax reporting (if accounts are restructured), or breach confidentiality.
- **Concession UBS will make:** Commit to "Chinese wall" during integration—Credit Suisse client advisors retained, client data not merged into UBS systems for 12 months.
- **What clients will do:** 10-15% will leave anyway (to Pictet, Julius Baer, Singapore banks). Majority will stay if UBS handles communication well.

### **AT1 Bondholders**
- **Objective:** Recover par or near-par; preserve capital-structure hierarchy (debt > equity).
- **Conflict with FINMA/Federal Council:** If deal structure involves AT1 write-down while equity gets UBS shares, bondholders will scream "illegal inversion" and sue.
- **Concession AT1 holders will receive:** Likely zero or token recovery (5-10%). Legal basis: AT1 prospectus viability-event clause triggered by SNB liquidity support (contractual, if aggressive interpretation).
- **What bondholders will do:** Litigate in Swiss courts and international arbitration. Likely multi-year process. Federal Council calculates: better to face lawsuits than systemic collapse.
