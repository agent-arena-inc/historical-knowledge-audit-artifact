2. **UBS is the only credible Swiss acquirer.** No foreign bank will move at weekend speed under Swiss regulatory complexity. No other domestic player has balance-sheet capacity for a CHF 500bn+ book.

   - Federal loss protection (CHF 5-10bn corridor)
   - SNB liquidity facility for combined entity
   - FINMA waiver on immediate capital add-ons
   - Material Adverse Change (MAC) clause carve-out for pre-signing deterioration

## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs. SECONDARY

### **TIER 1: DECISIVE (Any one can collapse the deal)**

#### **A. Weekend Execution Failure → Monday Bank Run**
- **Probability if no deal announced Sunday night:** 85%+
- **Mechanism:** Asian wealth clients (Singapore, Hong Kong booking centers) begin Monday withdrawal instructions. By European open, Credit Suisse Markets counterparties refuse to roll overnight funding. Intraday liquidity need could exceed CHF 50bn Monday alone.
- **Mitigation:** Announcement must include:
  - Named acquirer (UBS)
  - Specific government guarantee quantum
  - SNB "unlimited liquidity" language
  - FINMA statement that combined entity is well-capitalized

**This is the forcing function for the entire weekend.**

#### **B. UBS Board Veto**
- **Probability without government guarantees:** 60%
- **UBS shareholders have already suffered integration pain** (PaineWebber 2000, domestic retail mergers). Board fiduciary duty requires ironclad downside protection.
- **Critical concessions UBS will demand:**
  - **MAC protection:** If Credit Suisse deposits fall >CHF 30bn between Friday close and signing, UBS can reprice or withdraw
  - **Legacy asset quarantine:** Securitized Products Group, Archegos residual, any remaining Greensill exposure → ring-fenced with government first-loss
  - **Regulatory capital holiday:** 12-18 months before combined entity must meet full Swiss SIFI buffers
- **Mitigation:** Federal Council must grant emergency ordinance powers to override standard merger-review timelines and capital rules.

#### **C. AT1 Bondholder Legal Injunction (Switzerland/UK/Luxembourg)**
- **Outstanding AT1 notional:** ~CHF 16bn
- **If authorities write down AT1 to zero while equity receives anything:**
  - Violates standard creditor hierarchy (debt senior to equity)
  - Immediate legal challenges in multiple jurisdictions
  - **Could freeze the transaction** if a court grants interim relief
- **Decision:**
  - **Option 1 (Lower Legal Risk):** AT1 converts to equity at punitive ratio (e.g., 1:100), then equity gets acquired at low price → AT1 holders receive ~CHF 0.10–0.50 per CHF 100 face
  - **Option 2 (Higher Legal Risk):** Invoke FINMA's viability-event powers to write down AT1 to zero, argue equity value is regulatory forbearance not market value
  - **Option 3 (Expensive):** Partial AT1 consideration (CHF 2-4bn) to avoid litigation
  
**My recommendation:** Option 1. The legal risk of full write-down while equity survives is **unacceptable** for a weekend timeline. Convert AT1 to equity, then let the acquisition price crush both.

#### **D. Federal Council Political Fragility**
- **7-member executive, no single party majority.** Requires 4 votes for emergency ordinance.
- **SVP (right-populist) opposition likely:** "Why bail out bonus bankers?"
- **Greens/SP (left) opposition likely:** "Why protect wealth management for global rich?"
- **Critical persuasion:** Frame as "jobs and financial center" not "Credit Suisse rescue." Emphasize 50,000 Swiss jobs, pension fund exposures, derivatives market functioning.
- **Mitigation:** Federal Finance Minister (FDF) must secure 4 votes **Saturday evening** before detailed UBS negotiation. Cannot negotiate a deal that Federal Council then rejects.

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (Financial Market Supervisory Authority)**
**Objective:** Orderly resolution without taxpayer loss; preserve Swiss regulatory credibility internationally.

**Conflicts:**
- **vs. AT1 holders:** FINMA has legal power to write down AT1 under viability event, but international precedent (Banco Popular 2017) shows litigation risk.
- **vs. Credit Suisse equity holders:** Must communicate that equity value is effectively zero in resolution.
- **vs. UBS:** Wants UBS to absorb maximum risk; UBS demands government guarantees.

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Grants UBS regulatory capital relief** (12-18 month phase-in)
- **Accepts government guarantee as necessary** to secure UBS participation
- **On AT1:** Will likely support conversion-to-equity rather than outright write-down to minimize legal risk, **unless Federal Council overrules for cost reasons**

---

### **SNB (Swiss National Bank)**
**Objective:** Financial stability; prevent contagion to broader Swiss banking system; protect Swiss franc credibility.

**Conflicts:**
- **vs. Federal Council:** SNB can provide liquidity but not solvency support — insists fiscal authorities must backstop credit risk.
- **vs. UBS:** SNB will provide liquidity but only against eligible collateral; UBS will demand expanded collateral definitions for Credit Suisse legacy assets.

**Likely Concessions:**
- **CHF 100bn+ liquidity facility** to combined entity
- **Expanded collateral framework** for troubled Credit Suisse portfolios (e.g., accept securitized products at haircut)
- **Public statement of "unlimited liquidity"** to signal credibility

**Red Line:** SNB will not take credit risk onto its balance sheet without Federal guarantee.

---

### **Federal Council (Swiss Executive Government)**
**Objective:** Protect Swiss jobs, financial center reputation, avoid taxpayer loss if possible; minimize political backlash.

**Conflicts:**
- **vs. Taxpayers:** Any guarantee creates contingent liability.
- **vs. UBS:** UBS demands maximize taxpayer risk.
- **vs. AT1 holders:** Writing down AT1 saves CHF 16bn but creates international legal/political crisis.

**Likely Concessions:**
- **CHF 9-10bn federal loss guarantee** on Credit Suisse legacy portfolios (structured as first-loss, not unlimited)
- **Emergency ordinance** to override competition law, accelerate merger approval
- **On AT1:** **Critical decision point—see below**

**Political Calculation:**
- **If AT1 written down:** Save CHF 16bn, face international lawsuits, anger global investors
- **If AT1 converted to equity then crushed:** Lawful under capital structure, costs nothing extra (equity already worthless), minimal legal risk

**Expected:** Federal Council will support AT1 conversion to avoid legal quagmire, **unless FINMA/SNB insist full write-down is legally defensible and necessary to show "burden-sharing."**
