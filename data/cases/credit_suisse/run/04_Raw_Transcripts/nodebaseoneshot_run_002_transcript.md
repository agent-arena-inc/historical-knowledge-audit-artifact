## 1. DOMINANT DECISION PATH (Expected/Recommended)

### **Primary Recommendation: Forced Merger with UBS**

**Rationale:**
- Credit Suisse is experiencing a terminal confidence crisis. The Saudi National Bank refusal on Wednesday removed the last credible private-capital backstop.
- Standalone recapitalization is **not viable** within 62 hours—no investor will commit material capital to a franchise bleeding deposits and trading counterparties.
- A bridge bank or full nationalization would destroy Switzerland's reputation as a private-banking center and create massive fiscal exposure (CHF 500bn+ balance sheet).
- UBS is the **only** Swiss institution with the balance sheet, international credibility, and operational capacity to absorb Credit Suisse rapidly enough to stop the run.

**Critical Path (must execute by Sunday evening):**

**Friday Night → Saturday Morning:**
- Federal Council convenes emergency session with FINMA, SNB, UBS leadership
- SNB provides **immediate** liquidity backstop (CHF 50-100bn) to Credit Suisse to ensure Monday opening capability
- Parallel track: FINMA begins formal supervisory intervention under Banking Act Art. 25-26 (protective measures)

**Saturday:**
- UBS presented with three options: (a) acquire CS, (b) acquire CS Investment Bank only, (c) decline and face regulatory pressure
- Federal Council makes clear this is a **national stability imperative**—UBS's license to operate depends on cooperation
- Negotiating team established: UBS CEO/CFO, CS Chairman, FINMA Director, SNB Chairman, Federal Councillor (Finance)

**Sunday (critical 24 hours):**
- Price negotiation: UBS demands CS equity at CHF 0.50-1.00/share (Friday close ~CHF 1.86) given execution risk
- **Government concessions package:**
  - Loss protection: CHF 6-10bn government guarantee on specific CS asset portfolios (real estate, leveraged loans)
  - Liquidity facility: SNB committed funding lines (CHF 100bn+) for combined entity
  - Regulatory forbearance: capital ratio relief for 12-24 months on combined group
  - Antitrust waiver: fast-track approval despite 60%+ Swiss market share in wealth management
- **AT1 bondholder treatment:** Likely full or partial write-down to absorb losses and reduce UBS's capital burden—**this is the political lightning rod**
- Legal documentation sprint: merger agreement, government guarantee terms, SNB facility documentation
- Federal Council formal decision: ~18:00 Sunday
- Public announcement: 21:00-22:00 Sunday (before Asian markets open)

## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs. SECONDARY

### **DECISIVE RISKS (Any one can cause catastrophic failure):**

#### **R1: UBS Refuses or Demands Impossible Terms**
- **Probability: 30-40%**
- **Impact:** No Plan B exists. CS collapses Monday morning, triggering:
  - CHF 300-500bn deposit insurance claims (overwhelms Swiss guarantee fund)
  - Global derivatives market chaos (CS is major ISDA participant)
  - Contagion to other European banks already stressed (Deutsche Bank CDS already elevated)
- **Mitigation:** Federal Council must make UBS refusal politically/commercially untenable:
  - Explicit threat: "Your license depends on this"
  - Sweetener: Government absorbs first CHF 6-10bn of losses
  - Future benefit: Eliminate main Swiss competitor, consolidate wealth management oligopoly

#### **R2: Deposit Run Accelerates Over Weekend**
- **Probability: 60-70%**
- **Impact:** Even with SNB liquidity, if CHF 50-100bn flows out Friday-Monday, CS may be operationally unable to open branches/systems
- **Current intelligence:** Wealth clients pulling cash, hedge funds withdrawing prime brokerage balances, corporate treasurers moving operating accounts
- **Mitigation:** 
  - SNB must pre-position **unlimited** liquidity (announce "whatever it takes")
  - FINMA imposes temporary withdrawal restrictions (legally dubious, politically toxic)
  - Federal Council considers emergency bank holiday Monday (last resort—would confirm panic)

#### **R3: AT1 Bondholder Litigation Blocks Deal**
- **Probability: 40-50%**
- **Impact:** CHF 16bn AT1 instruments; if written down while equity gets UBS offer, bondholders claim violation of capital hierarchy
- **Legal exposure:** English law AT1s may seek UK injunction; Swiss law unclear on resolution hierarchy
- **Timing crisis:** Cannot wait for courts—must impose write-down via FINMA emergency order and litigate later
- **Mitigation:**
  - FINMA legal team prepares Art. 25-30 emergency order justifying AT1 write-down as "necessary to preserve financial stability"
  - Federal Council accepts 3-5 years of litigation and potential CHF 5-10bn settlement exposure
  - Alternative: Partial write-down (50-70%) to reduce bondholder fury while still protecting UBS

#### **R4: Political Revolt (Parliament/Public)**
- **Probability: 50-60%**
- **Impact:** Federal Council has emergency powers but faces Monday morning political firestorm:
  - Left: "Socializing losses, privatizing UBS monopoly profits"
  - Right: "Destroying shareholder property rights, bailout moral hazard"
  - Zürich vs. rest of Switzerland: "Why sacrifice taxpayers for bankers?"
- **Mitigation:** 
  - Frame as existential: "Without this, Swiss banking system collapses, CHF 200bn GDP loss, 100k jobs"
  - Demand UBS concessions: strict lending requirements for SMEs, branch network preservation
  - Extract penalties: CS senior management forfeit all unvested comp, clawback bonuses 2020-2023

### **SECONDARY RISKS (Manageable but painful):**

#### **R5: International Coordination Failure**
- ECB, Fed, BoE need to coordinate liquidity swaps and messaging
- China/Singapore regulators (major CS Asia hubs) may impose local restrictions
- **Mitigation:** SNB/FINMA hotline to BIS, Fed, ECB throughout weekend

#### **R6: UBS Operational Integration Chaos**
- Merging two global banks in 62 hours means Day 1 IT/operations failures likely
- **Mitigation:** Accept 2-4 weeks of client service disruption; prioritize payment systems over trading platforms

#### **R7: Credit Suisse Senior Management Obstruction**
- CS board may resist fire-sale terms, demand higher price for shareholders
- **Mitigation:** FINMA invokes supervisory powers, replaces board if necessary (Art. 25)
