## 1. DOMINANT DECISION PATH (Expected/Recommended)

### **Primary Recommendation: Forced Merger with UBS**

**Rationale:**
- Credit Suisse is experiencing catastrophic loss of market confidence with no private-sector rescue available (Saudi National Bank publicly declined further support on March 15)
- Regulatory statement of March 15 bought 48-72 hours maximum; market panic continues
- Standalone stabilization is not credible given structural governance failures (material weaknesses in internal controls, multi-year trust erosion)
- Switzerland cannot afford G-SIB failure; systemic risk to Swiss financial center is existential

**Concrete Steps for Weekend:**
1. **Friday night/Saturday morning:** Federal Council authorizes emergency merger framework under banking law
2. **Saturday:** FINMA/SNB present UBS with framework; negotiate:
   - Purchase price (likely symbolic, CHF 0.25-1.00 per share vs Friday close ~CHF 1.86)
   - Government loss-sharing on specific asset pools (investment banking, legacy litigation)
   - Liquidity backstop from SNB (CHF 100bn+ range)
   - Regulatory concessions (capital phase-in, competition law waiver)
3. **Sunday morning:** Finalize legal documentation
4. **Sunday evening:** Joint announcement by Federal Council/FINMA/SNB/UBS before Asian markets open

**Alternative Path (Lower Probability):**
Nationalization/temporary state ownership if UBS refuses terms, but this creates:
- Immediate sovereign credit concerns
- No clear exit strategy
- Potential EU state-aid complications
- Loss of operational expertise

## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### **DECISIVE RISKS (Deal-Breakers)**

#### **D1: UBS Refusal to Acquire**
- **Probability:** 40-50% without extraordinary government support
- **Impact:** Forces nationalization or uncontrolled Monday failure
- **Mitigation Required:** 
  - Government must absorb tail risks on worst asset pools (CHF 5-10bn guarantee)
  - Indemnify UBS management/board from future litigation
  - Waive competition review (UBS+CS = ~50% Swiss banking market)

#### **D2: Weekend Deposit Flight Accelerates**
- **Probability:** 70-80% if no announcement by Sunday evening
- **Current State:** Wealth Management clients (CHF 500bn+ AUM) already moving assets; corporate treasury relationships under review
- **Critical Threshold:** If institutional clients pre-position Monday redemptions/withdrawals over weekend, liquidity gap becomes unbridgeable even with SNB support
- **Mitigation Required:**
  - SNB must pre-commit unlimited liquidity (not just "if necessary" language)
  - Direct weekend outreach to top 50 clients by FINMA/Federal Council

#### **D3: AT1 Bondholder Legal Injunction**
- **Probability:** 30-40% if AT1 write-down attempted
- **Context:** CHF 16bn AT1 outstanding; contractual write-down provisions exist but unprecedented in going-concern merger
- **Impact:** Could delay Monday announcement, trigger broader debt market panic
- **Mitigation Required:**
  - Legal opinion that merger = viability event under prospectus terms
  - Coordinate with UK/EU regulators (many AT1 holders are European institutions)
  - Consider partial compensation or conversion to equity in merged entity

### **SECONDARY RISKS (Manageable)**

#### **S1: UBS Shareholder Revolt**
- UBS shareholders will oppose dilutive acquisition of troubled competitor
- **Management:** Emphasize monopoly rents post-merger, government guarantees limit downside

#### **S2: Political Backlash (Left/Right)**
- Left: Bailout of bankers, socialization of losses
- Right: Competition concerns, state intervention
- **Management:** Frame as preventing 50,000+ job losses, protecting Swiss Franc credibility

#### **S3: Cross-Border Regulatory Coordination**
- ECB, Fed, BoE need advance notice (CS has significant EU/US operations)
- **Management:** SNB/FINMA have weekend hotlines to peer regulators; this is procedural

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (Financial Regulator)**
**Primary Objective:** Orderly resolution, no market contagion, preserve regulatory credibility

**Conflicts:**
- vs. AT1 Bondholders: FINMA may invoke write-down to preserve taxpayer, but faces legal/market precedent risk
- vs. Credit Suisse Management: Must remove board/executives to satisfy public accountability
- vs. UBS: Needs UBS cooperation but cannot appear captured by private interests

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** Regulatory forbearance on UBS capital ratios (phase-in period for combined entity), fast-track approvals
- **Gets:** UBS commits to acquire; management accepts forced departure

---

### **SNB (Central Bank)**
**Primary Objective:** Financial stability, protect Swiss Franc, maintain inflation credibility

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Federal Council: SNB balance sheet expansion (liquidity support) creates inflation risk, but political pressure for bailout is intense
- vs. UBS: SNB must provide liquidity but wants private-sector risk absorption
- vs. Market: Any perceived "blank check" creates moral hazard

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** CHF 100-200bn liquidity line to merged entity (exceptional and temporary), accepts balance sheet expansion
- **Gets:** Government fiscal guarantee backing the liquidity (SNB not absorbing credit risk), commitment to unwind support within 24 months

---

### **Federal Council (Government)**
**Primary Objective:** Prevent systemic crisis, protect jobs/tax base, minimize political damage

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Taxpayers: Any fiscal guarantee is politically toxic, but failure is worse
- vs. UBS: Needs UBS to acquire but UBS demands extraordinary terms
- vs. AT1 Holders: Writing down AT1 while equity has residual value inverts capital structure norms, invites lawsuits

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** CHF 9-12bn loss guarantee on specific CS asset pools (non-core, litigation reserves), competition law emergency waiver
- **Gets:** UBS acquisition, private-sector solution narrative, avoids nationalization

---

### **Credit Suisse (Management/Board)**
**Primary Objective:** (At this point) Personal legal liability minimization, orderly exit

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Shareholders: Any merger will wipe out equity value (90%+ loss from current price)
- vs. Employees: Mass layoffs inevitable in merger
- vs. Own Legacy: Admission of catastrophic failure

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** Board resignation, cooperation with transition, acceptance of nominal sale price
- **Gets:** Legal indemnification for good-faith crisis decisions, severance packages (politically constrained)

---

### **UBS (Potential Acquirer)**
**Primary Objective:** Acquire only if upside exceeds risk; protect UBS franchise and shareholder value

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Federal Council/FINMA: UBS has leverage (only credible acquirer) but cannot appear to extort government
- vs. Own Shareholders: Deal must be accretive or heavily de-risked
- vs. CS Clients: Integration risk, culture clash in Wealth Management

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** Agrees to acquire at government-determined price, accepts short timeline
- **Gets:** 
  - Government loss-sharing on CHF 25-40bn of riskiest assets
  - SNB liquidity backstop
  - Regulatory capital relief
  - Competition authority waiver
  - Legal liability shield
  - Option to shed non-core CS assets quickly

---

### **Wealth Management Clients (Large)**
**Primary Objective:** Preserve capital, maintain access to funds, minimize disruption

**Conflicts:**
- vs. CS/Regulators: Want immediate withdrawal rights vs. need for orderly resolution
- vs. Time: Weekend creates forced waiting period, but Monday uncertainty is intolerable

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** Accept UBS as new relationship bank (if credible announcement)
- **Gets:** Explicit guarantee that deposits/assets are safe, continuity of service, UBS brand reassurance

---

### **AT1 Bondholders**
**Primary Objective:** Preserve bond value, enforce contractual seniority over equity

**Conflicts:**
- vs. FINMA/Federal Council: Regulators may write down AT1 to zero while equity retains value (contractually permitted under viability-event clause, but unprecedented in merger context)
- vs. Equity Holders: Fundamental capital structure dispute
- vs. Market Precedent: Write-down would devastate European AT1 market (€200bn+ outstanding)

**Likely Concessions:**
- **Gives:** (If write-down imposed) Accept total loss under legal duress
- **Gets:** (Best case) Partial conversion to equity in merged entity OR (worst case) legal right to challenge in court (but post-facto)

**CRITICAL DECISION:** This is the most legally and financially contentious issue. Recommend:
- **Option A (Lower Risk):** Convert AT1 to equity in merged entity (heavily diluted but non-zero)
- **Option B (Higher Risk):** Full write-down, accept litigation risk, rely on prospectus viability-event language
