## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### **DECISIVE RISKS** (any one can trigger catastrophic failure)

**D1. UBS Refusal / Deal Breakdown (Probability: 35%)**
- **Nature**: UBS walks away or demands impossible terms (full government assumption of all CS liabilities)
- **Why decisive**: No Plan B exists in the 48-hour window. Nationalization requires emergency legislation that cannot pass over a weekend.
- **Consequence**: Uncontrolled CS failure Monday morning, immediate CHF 500bn+ balance sheet in resolution, global systemic event
- **Mitigation**: Federal Council must make credible threat of legislative intervention (temporary nationalization law) to force UBS cooperation

**D2. Weekend Bank Run Acceleration (Probability: 60%)**
- **Nature**: Wealth Management clients execute standing instructions; institutional counterparties pull credit lines; digital channels enable 24/7 withdrawals
- **Why decisive**: CS operating liquidity could fall below critical threshold (est. CHF 30–40bn minimum) before deal closes
- **Consequence**: Forced FINMA resolution action mid-weekend, negotiation leverage collapses entirely
- **Mitigation**: 
  - SNB must provide unlimited overnight liquidity Friday→Saturday (CHF 50–100bn immediate)
  - FINMA prepares emergency moratorium authority (legally questionable but may be necessary)
  - Coordinate with SIX Swiss Exchange for Monday trading halt if needed

**D3. AT1 Bondholder Legal Injunction (Probability: 25%)**
- **Nature**: €17bn AT1 holders seek emergency court orders blocking any transaction that impairs them while equity receives value
- **Why decisive**: Swiss/UK/US courts could issue weekend injunctions that prevent deal closure
- **Consequence**: Deal collapse, Monday chaos
- **Mitigation**: 
  - Legal team must prepare defensive brief on viability-event contractual triggers
  - Consider full AT1 write-down to zero (contractually defensible under prospectus terms) to eliminate class entirely before merger
  - This is legally and politically explosive but may be necessary for clean transaction

### **SECONDARY RISKS** (manageable with proper planning)

**S1. Political Opposition (Probability: 70%, but not blocking)**
- Left parties will demand employee protections, branch network commitments
- Right parties will object to government guarantees
- **Management**: Federal Council invokes emergency economic powers; Parliamentary review comes *after* the rescue

**S2. EU/ECB Coordination Failure (Probability: 40%)**
- European regulators may resist UBS absorption of CS European entities
- **Management**: SNB/FINMA direct engagement with Bundesbank, ECB, BoE Friday night; accept minor divestitures if necessary

**S3. Wealth Client Tax Concerns (Probability: 80%)**

- **Management**: UBS commits to maintaining separate CS wealth entity for 12–24 months; not a weekend issue

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA vs SNB vs Federal Council**

**Conflict:**
- **FINMA**: Wants clean prudential resolution, willing to impose losses on all investors including AT1 write-down
- **SNB**: Prioritizes financial stability, fears contagion to Swiss banking system, willing to provide massive liquidity but resists open-ended fiscal commitment
- **Federal Council**: Faces political accountability, wants to minimize taxpayer exposure and avoid appearance of "bailout," but terrified of Switzerland losing G-SIB status

**Resolution:**
- **FINMA concedes**: Accepts merger over formal resolution (preserves going-concern, minimizes immediate disruption)
- **SNB concedes**: Provides CHF 100–200bn liquidity (unprecedented scale) but demands Federal guarantee for potential losses
- **Federal Council concedes**: Approves CHF 6–10bn guarantee but extracts commitment from UBS on jobs/Swiss operations (political cover)

---

### **UBS (potential acquirer) vs Swiss Authorities**

**Conflict:**
- **UBS position**: "We didn't create this mess, why should we risk our shareholders and franchise?" Demands full downside protection, Cherry-picking assets unacceptable in 48-hour timeline
- **Authorities position**: "You're the only option, and you'll be the monopoly Swiss G-SIB afterward. National interest requires your cooperation."

**UBS's negotiating demands:**
1. Purchase price: Symbolic only (CHF 0.25–0.50/share, ~CHF 1–2bn total)
2. Government loss protection: CHF 8–12bn for specified assets
3. SNB liquidity: CHF 150–200bn for combined entity transition
4. Regulatory forbearance: 18–24 months to meet combined capital requirements
5. Antitrust immunity: Pre-clearance from all relevant jurisdictions
6. Legal indemnity: Protection from CS legacy litigation

**Likely outcome:**
- **UBS gets**: Points 1, 2 (at lower end), 3, 5, 6 (partial)
- **UBS concedes**: Accepts some non-core CS businesses it would prefer to shed; commits to 12-month employment protection for critical CS staff
- **Key leverage point**: Federal Council credibly signals it will pursue nationalization legislation if UBS refuses (destroys UBS's negotiating position as "only option")

---

### **AT1 Bondholders vs Equity Holders vs Authorities**

**Conflict:**
- **AT1 holders** (~CHF 17bn): "We're senior to equity in capital structure. Any deal that gives equity value while we're wiped out is illegal."
- **Equity holders**: Desperate for any recovery (currently trading ~CHF 1.80/share)
- **FINMA**: AT1 prospectus contains viability-event write-down language; argues this is contractually valid

**This is the most legally dangerous conflict:**

Traditional capital hierarchy: Equity → AT1 → T2 → Senior debt. But AT1 contracts contain "going concern loss absorption" triggers that may permit write-down while bank continues operating.

**Likely outcome (two scenarios):**

*Scenario A (60% probability):* **Full AT1 write-down before merger**
- FINMA declares viability event Friday night/Saturday morning
- Writes AT1 to zero under contractual terms
- UBS then acquires CS with clean Tier 1 capital structure
- Equity receives nominal value (CHF 0.50–1.00/share)
- **Risk**: Global AT1 market seizes up; immediate legal challenges in multiple jurisdictions; political firestorm

*Scenario B (40% probability):* **AT1 converted to equity, then diluted**
- AT1 converted to shares at punitive ratio
- UBS acquisition then massively dilutes all equity (including converted AT1)
- Both classes suffer but hierarchy preserved
- **Risk**: More complex, harder to execute in 48 hours; may not satisfy UBS's demand for clean structure

**Key decision point**: FINMA/SNB must decide **Saturday morning** whether to trigger AT1 write-down. This decision alone could trigger €200bn+ in global AT1 repricing.

---

### **Large Wealth Clients vs All Other Stakeholders**

**Conflict:**
- **Wealth clients**: Want absolute confidentiality, immediate access to funds, preservation of relationship-banking model
- **Authorities/UBS**: Need to stabilize deposit base, prevent weekend run, integrate systems

**Likely outcome:**
- **Clients concede**: Accept UBS merger (no choice)
- **UBS concedes**: 
  - Maintains separate CS private banking brand for 12–24 months
  - Guarantees existing relationship managers stay in place
  - Commits to gradual system integration (not forced migration)
- **Authorities concede**: No weekend withdrawal restrictions (too legally problematic in Switzerland), but SNB provides unlimited liquidity to accommodate outflows
