# CRISIS ADVISORY ANALYSIS — CREDIT SUISSE
**As of Friday 17 March 2023, 2100 CET**

---

## 1. DOMINANT DECISION PATH (Expected/Recommended)

### Primary Recommendation: **Forced Merger with UBS over the Weekend**

**Rationale:**
- **Monday reopening is the hard constraint.** With Saudi National Bank's refusal triggering ~25-30% share collapse and CDS at distressed levels, market confidence has evaporated. Any sign of hesitation Monday morning will trigger catastrophic deposit flight.
- **Standalone recapitalization is dead.** No private investor will step in after the largest shareholder publicly walked away. A rights issue would take weeks and fail in current market conditions.
- **Temporary nationalization carries unacceptable contagion risk.** Switzerland's credibility as a global wealth management hub depends on private-sector solutions. A state takeover would signal systemic failure and trigger capital flight across the entire Swiss banking sector.

**Expected Structure (to be negotiated this weekend):**
- UBS acquires Credit Suisse in an all-share transaction at a significant discount to current market price
- Swiss government provides:
  - **Loss protection guarantee** (CHF 5-10bn range) to cover worst-case legacy positions
  - **Liquidity backstop** via SNB (CHF 100bn+ facility) to stem deposit flight during transition
  - **Regulatory forbearance** on capital ratios during integration (12-18 months)
- Transaction announced Sunday evening before Asian markets open

## 2. KEY RISKS — DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### **DECISIVE RISKS** (any one can sink the entire rescue)

**D1: UBS Refuses / Demands Unacceptable Terms**
- *Likelihood:* MODERATE-HIGH
- *Impact:* CATASTROPHIC
- UBS has no commercial incentive to acquire a failing competitor. Board will demand massive government subsidies and legal protections.
- **Decision point:** How much taxpayer money/risk is politically survivable? Federal Council must decide maximum loss guarantee (likely CHF 8-12bn) before approaching UBS.
- If UBS walks away, only options are emergency nationalization or chaotic resolution Monday morning.

**D2: Wealth Client Run Accelerates Before Announcement**
- *Likelihood:* HIGH
- *Impact:* CATASTROPHIC
- Credit Suisse's ~CHF 500bn+ wealth management book is the crown jewel but extremely flight-prone.
- Asian clients (largest segment) have 48 hours over the weekend to move money before Monday.
- **Mitigation required:** Confidential calls to top 50 ultra-high-net-worth clients (aggregate >CHF 100bn) by CEO/Chairman Friday night through Sunday, offering personal guarantees of continuity.
- If >CHF 20-30bn flees before announcement, acquirer may abandon deal.

**D3: AT1 Bondholder Legal Injunction**
- *Likelihood:* MODERATE
- *Impact:* CRITICAL
- ~CHF 16bn AT1 bonds outstanding. Contractual write-down triggers exist under viability-event language.
- If FINMA determines government support constitutes such an event, AT1 could be written down while equity retains value in merger.
- **Problem:** This violates normal capital hierarchy (equity should be wiped first). AT1 holders will immediately seek injunctions in Swiss/UK/US courts.
- **Decision:** Can rescue proceed with AT1 write-down (cleaner balance sheet but massive litigation risk) or must AT1 be preserved (higher cost to acquirer)?

### **SECONDARY RISKS** (manageable but require active mitigation)

**S1: Swiss Political Backlash**
- Parliament is not in session; Federal Council can act unilaterally under emergency powers.
- Left parties will attack any taxpayer-funded "bailout of bankers."
- Right/business parties will attack UBS monopoly creation.
- **Mitigation:** Frame as "jobs and depositor protection," emphasize private-sector solution (UBS pays something, even if token amount).

**S2: EU/US Regulatory Approval Timing**
- UBS + Credit Suisse creates institution with ~CHF 1.6trn assets, major EU/US operations.
- Competition authorities normally require 6-12 month review.
- **Mitigation:** Emergency consultations Friday night with ECB, Fed, UK PRA to pre-clear weekend deal under financial-stability exception.

**S3: Credit Suisse Investment Bank Counterparty Freeze**
- If derivatives counterparties refuse to novate trades or demand additional collateral, IB becomes unmanageable.
- **Mitigation:** SNB provides unlimited liquidity facility Friday night to meet any margin calls; FINMA contacts top 20 counterparties to confirm business-as-usual treatment.

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS AND LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **FINMA (Financial Regulator)**
**Position:**
- Legal mandate: protect depositors and financial stability.
- Preference: Private-sector solution that preserves Switzerland's regulatory credibility.
- **Red line:** Will not allow Credit Suisse to open Monday without credible rescue.

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Federal Council: FINMA wants AT1 write-down to clean balance sheet; Federal Council fears legal/political blowback.
- vs. SNB: FINMA wants to minimize taxpayer exposure; SNB willing to provide unlimited liquidity to prevent contagion.

**Likely concessions:**
- Accepts equity holders retain some value (even if "unfair" vs AT1) to get deal done.
- Provides immediate regulatory capital relief to merged entity.

---

### **SNB (Central Bank)**
**Position:**
- Mandate: monetary and financial stability.
- Willing to provide functionally unlimited liquidity (this is their superpower).
- **Red line:** Will not allow disorderly collapse that threatens Swiss franc credibility.

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Federal Council: SNB wants government to provide loss guarantees; Federal Council wants SNB to bear more risk via liquidity only.
- vs. UBS: SNB wants UBS to pay meaningful consideration; UBS wants gift price.

**Likely concessions:**
- Provides CHF 100bn+ liquidity facility (possibly non-recourse in practice).
- Accepts that some liquidity may never be repaid, disguising it as temporary.

---

### **Federal Council (Government)**
**Position:**
- Political survival depends on preventing both bank collapse AND voter anger over bailouts.
- Ideally: private UBS deal with minimal visible taxpayer cost.
- **Red line:** Cannot be seen as repeating 2008 UBS bailout (CHF 68bn, politically toxic for a decade).

**Conflicts:**
- vs. UBS: Council needs deal done; UBS has veto power and will extort maximum guarantees.
- vs. AT1 holders: Writing down AT1 saves money but creates legal chaos and sets bad precedent for Swiss financial instruments globally.

**Likely concessions:**
- Provides CHF 8-10bn loss guarantee (structured to look like "insurance" not bailout).
- Agrees to indemnify UBS board members against shareholder lawsuits.
- Accepts UBS monopoly in Swiss banking (no alternative).

---

### **Credit Suisse (Management/Board)**
**Position:**
- CEO/Chairman are essentially powerless; decisions are being made around them.
- Only goal: preserve personal reputation and avoid criminal liability for misrepresentation.

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Everyone: Management will be blamed for crisis regardless of outcome.

**Likely concessions:**
- Board will resign as condition of any deal.
- Management accepts immediate termination in exchange for separation packages and no prosecution.

---

### **UBS (Potential Acquirer)**
**Position:**
- **Leverage:** Only credible private-sector acquirer; can demand almost anything.
- Commercial logic: Credit Suisse wealth management business is valuable, but investment bank is toxic and integration will cost CHF 5-10bn over 3-5 years.
- **Red line:** Will not proceed without government absorbing tail risks (litigation, legacy positions, client defections).

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Everyone else: UBS wants maximum subsidies, minimum price, full protection.
- Internal conflict: UBS CEO may personally favor deal (empire building) but board members fear integration risk and shareholder dilution.

**Likely concessions:**
- Pays token amount (CHF 2-4bn in shares, representing 60-80% discount to Friday closing price).
- Accepts some reputational risk of being "bailout beneficiary."
- Commits to preserving X thousand Swiss jobs for Y years (political theatre).

---

### **Large Wealth Clients**
**Position:**
- Want absolute certainty their assets are safe and accessible Monday morning.
- Will move money instantly to JP Morgan, HSBC, Julius Baer if any doubt.
- **Red line:** Cannot tolerate any freeze, even temporary.

**Conflicts:**
- vs. Credit Suisse/acquirer: Need immediate clarity but deal terms may not be final.

**Likely concessions:**
- Accept UBS as new custodian (even if they preferred Credit Suisse relationship bankers).
- Will demand fee concessions and enhanced terms as price of staying.

---

### **AT1 Bondholders (CHF 16bn outstanding)**
**Position:**
- Contractually subordinate to depositors/senior debt but senior to equity.
- Prospectus allows write-down under viability event, BUT normal expectation is equity wiped first.
- Major holders: Swiss pension funds, international asset managers, hedge funds.

**Conflicts:**
- vs. FINMA/Federal Council: If AT1 written down while equity holders get merger consideration, will immediately litigate.
- vs. UBS: Cleaner balance sheet if AT1 absorbed losses, but creates precedent problem for all Swiss AT1s.

**Likely concessions:**
- If write-down occurs: Accept it but file lawsuits (takes years, uncertain outcome).
- If preserved: Accept significant haircut in exchange for UBS AT1 instruments or cash at 60-70% of par.
