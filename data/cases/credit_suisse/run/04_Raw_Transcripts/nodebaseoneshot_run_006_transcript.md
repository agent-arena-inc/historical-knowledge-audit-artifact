## 2. RISK MATRIX: DECISIVE vs SECONDARY

### DECISIVE RISKS (Any one triggers catastrophic outcome):

**D1: Weekend Liquidity Death Spiral** [PROBABILITY: 75% without intervention]
- **Mechanics:** Even with Wednesday's SNB statement, institutional clients and trading counterparties continue rapid pullback Friday → weekend → Monday
- **Trigger:** CS needs ~CHF 10-20bn liquidity **daily** just to meet ongoing withdrawal requests
- **Consequence:** Without massive SNB support, CS cannot open Monday; forced resolution in chaos
- **Why decisive:** No solution survives a liquidity-driven collapse before announcement
- **Mitigation:** SNB must have unlimited liquidity facility active by Saturday morning

**D2: UBS Refuses / Demands Impossible Terms** [PROBABILITY: 40%]
- **Mechanics:** UBS Board balks at integration risk, legacy litigation, wealth-client conflicts
- **Trigger:** UBS demands government guarantees > Federal Council political tolerance (e.g., CHF 20bn+)
- **Consequence:** No private-sector solution → nationalization (politically toxic) or disorderly wind-down
- **Why decisive:** No other buyer exists; Big 4 auditor won't bless standalone restructuring in 48 hours
- **Mitigation:** Federal Council must pre-commit to "whatever it takes" loss guarantee

**D3: AT1/Debt Holder Legal Injunction** [PROBABILITY: 25%]
- **Mechanics:** ~CHF 16bn AT1 outstanding; if deal structure imposes losses, holders seek Swiss/UK court emergency injunction Sunday
- **Trigger:** Merger terms that write down AT1 while equity receives anything, or unclear loss-allocation
- **Consequence:** Deal completion blocked; CS opens Monday in legal limbo
- **Why decisive:** Cannot execute forced merger with capital structure under injunction
- **Mitigation:** Either (a) keep AT1 whole in acquisition, or (b) use emergency ordinance to override contractual rights (legally aggressive, invites litigation but allows Monday completion)

### SECONDARY RISKS (Manageable with proper execution):

**S1: Wealth Client Flight Accelerates** [IMPACT: High, but not weekend-critical]
- Wealthy clients moving assets to UBS/Julius Baer ongoing, but weekend timing limits execution
- Mitigation: UBS announcement actually stabilizes (clients prefer "rescued by strong bank" to uncertainty)

**S2: CS Employee Exodus** [IMPACT: Medium-term]
- Key bankers/traders jump to competitors
- Mitigation: UBS retention packages; unavoidable but doesn't block weekend deal

**S3: Swiss Political Backlash** [IMPACT: Medium]
- Left parties attack "bailout"; right attacks "forced marriage"
- Mitigation: Frame as "financial stability" not "rescue"; accept political cost

**S4: International Coordination Failure** [IMPACT: Low]
- Fed, ECB, BoE need heads-up but won't block Swiss sovereign decision
- Mitigation: SNB/FINMA Saturday calls to Basel/Frankfurt/Washington

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS & LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### FINMA vs Federal Council
**Conflict:** FINMA wants clean legal basis (slow); Federal Council wants speed
**Resolution:** Federal Council issues **emergency ordinance** under Art. 184(3) Constitution
- FINMA gets legal cover for forced merger without shareholder vote
- Federal Council accepts FINMA retains ongoing supervision of combined entity
**Concession:** FINMA swallows reputational risk of "regulatory failure" narrative

### SNB vs Federal Council (Fiscal Risk)
**Conflict:** SNB willing to provide liquidity (monetary policy); resists taking credit risk (quasi-fiscal)
**Resolution:** 
- SNB provides CHF 100-150bn **liquidity** facility (secured lending, temporary)
- Federal **government** provides CHF 9-10bn **loss guarantee** (fiscal, permanent risk)
**Concession:** SNB accepts reputational risk of "lender of last resort" becoming "lender of only resort"

### UBS vs Federal Council (Deal Terms)
**Conflict:** UBS wants full downside protection; Federal Council wants to minimize taxpayer exposure

**UBS demands:**
1. Government covers first CHF 9bn of losses on CS legacy portfolio
2. Regulatory capital relief (treat acquisition as merger, not new risk-weighted assets)
3. Antitrust fast-track approval (combined entity ~50% Swiss banking market)
4. Right to terminate if losses exceed CHF 15bn

**Federal Council counters:**
1. CHF 9bn guarantee accepted (necessary evil)
2. Capital relief approved (systemic stability trumps Basel rules)
3. Antitrust review deferred 12 months (emergency powers)
4. Termination right capped at CHF 12bn; UBS must retain CS Swiss unit

**Final concession balance:**
- UBS accepts **CHF 0.76/share purchase price** (total ~CHF 3bn) — humiliating for CS shareholders but UBS Board needs "bargain" optics
- Federal Council accepts **"too big to fail" becomes "even bigger to fail"** problem
- Both accept reputational damage (UBS = opportunistic; Federal = interventionist)

### Credit Suisse Board/Management vs Everyone
**Conflict:** CS Board wants standalone survival; all other stakeholders want immediate sale

**Pressure points:**
- FINMA: Threatens emergency administrator appointment if Board doesn't cooperate
- SNB: Makes liquidity conditional on merger progress
- Federal Council: Personal calls from Finance Minister to Chairman (Lehmann) — "national interest"

**CS concessions:**
- Board **unanimously recommends** sale to shareholders (kabuki theater; emergency ordinance makes vote moot)
- CEO Koerner steps aside immediately (UBS demands clean break)
- CS abandons all restructuring plans ("radical restructuring" announced Nov 2022 now irrelevant)

**What CS gets:**
- Orderly process (avoid TBTF liquidation)
- Some employees retained
- Wealth Management brand possibly survives under UBS (Swiss Universal Bank division)

### AT1 Bondholders vs FINMA/Federal Council
**Conflict:** AT1 holders expect equity wiped before AT1 touched; government wants cleanest deal

**Three options under discussion (Friday-Saturday):**

**Option A: Keep AT1 whole in UBS assumption**
- UBS assumes all CS liabilities including AT1
- Clean, legally simple
- Cost: UBS demands higher government guarantee (~CHF 12-15bn)

**Option B: Write down AT1 to zero, equity gets nominal value**
- Invokes AT1 "viability event" contractual terms (prospectus allows write-down under official support)
- Saves UBS ~CHF 16bn
- **Legal risk:** Violates normal capital hierarchy (equity junior to AT1); invites litigation
- **Regulatory logic:** "Going concern" write-down mechanism triggered by forced merger = viability event

**Option C: Write down both AT1 and equity**
- Cleanest hierarchy
- Cost: UBS won't pay anything for equity if AT1 also zeroed

**Likely outcome (60% probability): Option B**
- FINMA legal opinion: Forced merger = viability event under AT1 terms
- AT1 written to zero; equity gets CHF 0.50-0.76/share
- **Concession from AT1 holders:** None — imposed by regulatory fiat
- **Risk accepted:** Years of litigation (Credit Suisse AT1 holders vs FINMA/Swiss Confederation)
- **Why chosen:** Saves government ~CHF 5-7bn in guarantees; UBS won't proceed otherwise

### Wealth Clients vs New UBS-CS Entity
**Conflict:** Clients want stability; fear integration chaos

**Implicit deal:**
- UBS commits to **"Chinese wall" wealth management integration** (18-month gradual process)
- Key relationship managers retained with bonuses
- Client assets frozen over weekend (prevent Sunday wire transfers)
**Concession from clients:** Accept temporary illiquidity; trust UBS brand
