# USMCA Negotiation Analysis
## Senior Trade-Policy Assessment, 27 August 2018

---

## 1. EXPECTED DOMINANT PACKAGE

**Base Case (65% probability): Trilateral deal by mid-September 2018**

The most likely outcome is a **Canada-joins package** incorporating the U.S.-Mexico bilateral framework with targeted Canadian modifications. Key drivers:

- **Deadline pressure**: The outgoing Mexican administration (Peña Nieto) needs a signed text before December 1st transition; the U.S. wants to use fast-track authority requiring 90-day Congressional notice
- **Structural asymmetry**: The bilateral already exists; Canada faces "take the framework or walk away" leverage
- **Political necessity**: All three governments need a win; complete NAFTA collapse carries unacceptable economic/political costs

**Expected core elements:**
- **Autos**: ~75% RVC + labor-value-content wage floor (locked via bilateral)
- **Labor**: Enforceable standards with some mechanism beyond traditional state-to-state dispute settlement
- **Dairy**: Limited but politically-meaningful U.S. access increase to Canadian market (3-5% of market)
- **Chapter 19**: **Survives in modified or preserved form** — this is Canada's hardest red line
- **Sunset**: Replaced by periodic **review mechanism** (not automatic termination) — compromise between U.S. leverage demand and business certainty needs

**Alternative scenario (25% probability): Talks extend into October with heightened brinkmanship**

**Failure scenario (10% probability): Bilateral-only U.S.-Mexico deal**
- Would require U.S. willingness to fracture North American integration
- Faces severe Congressional opposition (see Section 4)

## 2. NEGOTIATION BOTTLENECKS & DOWNSTREAM RISKS

### DECISIVE BOTTLENECKS (deal-breakers)

#### A. **Chapter 19 Dispute Settlement** ⚠️ CRITICAL
**Nature of conflict:**
- USTR objective: explicit elimination
- Canada position: existential red line (protects against U.S. trade remedy abuse, especially softwood lumber)

**Why decisive:**
- Canada cannot sell elimination domestically; Freeland has staked political capital
- No viable middle ground between "exists" and "doesn't exist"
- Historical: Canada walked away from FTA talks in 1987 over this exact issue

**Likely resolution path:**
- U.S. **tactical retreat**: preserves Chapter 19 in exchange for other gains
- Face-saving: procedural modifications, transparency improvements, or parallel commitments on trade remedy practice
- **Risk if unresolved**: talks collapse or Canada excluded (unacceptable to Congressional Democrats, auto industry)

#### B. **Dairy Market Access** ⚠️ CRITICAL
**Nature of conflict:**
- U.S. agriculture/Wisconsin politically powerful; demands "real" access
- Canada supply management politically untouchable in Quebec, Ontario rural ridings

**Why decisive:**
- Trump has personally attacked Canadian dairy; needs tangible win
- Trudeau government cannot survive full capitulation
- Wisconsin = swing state; Quebec = Trudeau's base

**Likely resolution path:**
- **Calibrated concession**: Canada grants limited TRQ expansion (Class 6/7 ultra-filtered milk, fluid milk access)
- Sized to give U.S. political trophy without destroying Canadian system (~3.5-5% market access)
- **Risk**: Insufficient U.S. access → Congressional agriculture opposition; excessive access → Canadian government falls

### SECONDARY BOTTLENECKS (significant but resolvable)

#### C. **Sunset/Review Mechanism**
**Conflict:**
- U.S. wants leverage tool (5-year automatic sunset initially proposed)
- Business/Canada/Mexico need investment certainty

**Resolution path (high confidence):**
- Periodic review (every 5-6 years) with **option to extend** rather than automatic termination
- Longer initial term (12-16 years)
- Splits the difference; business accepts reviews if default is continuation

#### D. **Labor Enforcement Mechanism**
**Conflict:**
- U.S. labor demands **facility-specific, rapid enforcement** beyond traditional state-to-state
- Mexico sovereignty concerns about intrusive monitoring

**Resolution path:**
- Novel **rapid-response mechanism** for specific facilities
- Mexico accepts in exchange for capacity-building assistance, clear procedures
- Canada likely joins to maintain symmetry

**Downstream risk:** Implementation disputes, especially early test cases in Mexico

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS, CONCESSIONS & RED LINES

### USTR (Robert Lighthizer)

**Objectives (priority order):**
1. Enforceable labor standards (personal priority, legacy goal)
2. Higher auto RVC + wage floors (already achieved with Mexico)
3. Dairy access (Presidential directive)
4. Chapter 19 elimination (stated objective, but negotiable)
5. Sunset clause (leverage tool)

**Red lines:**
- Cannot return with "NAFTA-lite" — needs demonstrable wins on labor, autos, agriculture
- Must be sellable as "rebalancing" trade

**Likely concessions:**
- Chapter 19 (tactical retreat if gets labor + dairy)
- Sunset clause hardness (accepts review mechanism)

### Canada (Chrystia Freeland)

**Red lines (non-negotiable):**
1. **Chapter 19 preservation** (existential)
2. **Supply management system survival** (political survival)

**Negotiable (with limits):**
- Limited dairy access (if system preserved)
- Auto content rules (already integrated supply chains)
- Labor standards (Canada already compliant)

**Concessions Canada will make:**
- Dairy: 3-5% market access via new TRQs
- Autos: Accept 75% RVC + wage floors (hurts less than Mexico)
- Procedural: Chapter 19 transparency improvements
- Symbolic: cultural exemption modifications

**Strategy:**
- Use "last to join" position to preserve core interests
- Trade dairy access for Chapter 19 preservation
- Accept U.S.-Mexico auto framework as fait accompli

### Mexico (Ildefonso Guajardo / incoming López Obrador team)

**Position:**
- **Locked in** via bilateral; wants Canada to join quickly
- Accepted labor reforms (independent unions, collective bargaining)
- Nervous about enforcement intrusiveness but committed

**Red line:**
- Avoid reopening bilateral terms (auto RVC, labor commitments)

**Concession already made:**
- Significant labor law reforms (historically unprecedented)

### U.S. Congress / Labor (AFL-CIO, UAW)

**Requirements for ratification support:**
1. **Enforceable labor standards** with teeth (not aspirational text)
2. Mechanism to address Mexican wage suppression
3. No weakening of Chapter 19 (odd alliance with Canada)

**Red lines:**
- Will not support bilateral-only (breaks North American integration)
- Will not support weak labor enforcement

**Key dynamic:**
- Democratic support REQUIRED for passage (Republican free-trade skeptics)
- Labor unions = essential Democratic constituency
- Pelosi/Schumer need labor sign-off

### Auto Industry (Detroit Three, suppliers)

**Position:**
- **Deeply ambivalent** about 75% RVC + wage floors
- Compliance costs significant; supply chain disruption risk
- Prefer certainty over continued uncertainty

**Red lines:**
- Cannot accept sunset clause (kills long-cycle investment)
- Need adequate phase-in period (3-5 years)

**Likely stance:**
- Reluctant acceptance if review mechanism replaces sunset
- Will lobby for implementation flexibility

### U.S. Dairy/Agriculture

**Requirements:**
- Tangible Canadian market access
- Politically visible win (Wisconsin, New York)

**Will accept:**
- Partial access if framed as "breakthrough"
- TRQ expansion in specific classes

### North American Manufacturers (broader supply chains)

**Position:**
- Prefer NAFTA preservation over disruption
- Accept modest rules tightening for certainty

**Concern:**
- Compliance cost of complex new rules
- Sunset/review uncertainty

## 4. DOMESTIC RATIFICATION RISKS

### U.S. Congressional Ratification (HIGHEST RISK)

**Structural challenge:**
- Requires **both** Republican skeptics of trade deals AND Democrats demanding labor enforcement
- Narrow path: satisfy labor (Democratic votes) without losing too many Republicans

**Critical requirements:**

1. **Labor enforcement credibility**
   - Facility-specific rapid-response mechanism
   - AFL-CIO/UAW at minimum neutrality, ideally support
   - **Risk**: If labor opposes, Democratic votes disappear

2. **Chapter 19 preservation**
   - Key Democratic senators (Brown, Casey, Baldwin) + Canadian alliance
   - Elimination would cost Democratic votes
   - **Risk**: USTR stated objective vs. Congressional reality

3. **No hard sunset**
   - Business community will actively oppose automatic termination
   - Chamber of Commerce, NAM essential for Republican votes
   - **Risk**: Investment uncertainty = business opposition = Republican defections

4. **Dairy access sufficient for agriculture state senators**
   - Wisconsin (Johnson, Baldwin), New York (Schumer)
   - **Risk**: Insufficient access = agriculture opposition

**Ratification timeline risk:**
- 2019 = divided Congress (likely Democratic House)
- Pelosi as Speaker will demand labor/environmental improvements
- **Probable outcome**: Renegotiation of enforcement annexes in 2019 before ratification

**Vote math (illustrative):**
- Need ~60 Senate votes (avoid filibuster risk)
- Need 218 House votes
- Path: 120-140 Democrats (if labor supports) + 80-100 Republicans (if business accepts)

### Canadian Ratification (MODERATE RISK)

**Structure:**
- Parliamentary system = easier IF Trudeau maintains confidence
- Liberal minority/majority can pass with NDP support

**Requirements:**
- Chapter 19 preserved (essential)
- Supply management system intact (essential)
- Framed as "standing up to Trump" while preserving access

**Risk factors:**
- If dairy concessions too large: rural Quebec/Ontario backlash
- If seen as capitulation: political damage
- **Timing**: 2019 election year pressure

**Likely outcome:** Passes if core red lines held

### Mexican Ratification (LOW RISK)

**Structure:**
- MORENA supermajority under López Obrador (December 2018+)
- AMLO has signaled support for concluded deal

**Risk:**
- Labor enforcement seen as sovereignty infringement
- Nationalist backlash to wage-floor requirements

**Likely outcome:** Passes with AMLO support

## SYNTHESIS: CRITICAL PATH FORWARD

### Immediate (September 2018): Canada Joins

**Deal structure:**
- Canada accepts U.S.-Mexico auto framework (75% RVC + wage floors)
- Canada grants limited dairy access (3.5-4.5% market)
- **U.S. preserves Chapter 19** (critical concession)
- Review mechanism replaces sunset (16-year term, 6-year reviews with extension option)
- Facility-specific labor enforcement mechanism

**Key trade:**
- U.S. gets: labor enforcement, dairy access, higher auto content
- Canada gets: Chapter 19, supply management survival, market access preservation
- Mexico gets: deal conclusion, predictability

### Near-term (Late 2018-Early 2019): Signature and Submission

**Process:**
- Sign by November 30, 2018 (Mexican transition deadline)
- Submit to Congress with 90-day notice
- Implementation legislation drafted

### Medium-term (2019): Ratification Crisis

**HIGHEST RISK PERIOD:**

Democrats take House (November 2018 elections likely outcome); Pelosi demands modifications:

1. **Labor enforcement strengthening**
   - More aggressive rapid-response mechanism
   - Elimination of investor-state dispute settlement (ISDS) or severe restrictions
   - Environmental enforcement parity

2. **Pharmaceutical provisions**
   - Biologics exclusivity period (not in current analysis but will emerge)

**Expected outcome:**
- **Side letters or Protocol of Amendment** (spring/summer 2019)
- Strengthened enforcement, ISDS modifications
- AFL-CIO brought to "yes"
- Ratification late 2019/early 2020

### Downstream Risks (2020+)

1. **Implementation disputes**
   - First rapid-response labor cases test mechanism credibility
   - Auto RVC compliance challenges
   - Dairy TRQ administration disputes

2. **Political sustainability**
   - 2020 U.S. election: new administration may revisit
   - Canadian election (2019): new government may challenge
   - Mexican implementation: capacity questions

3. **Economic effectiveness**
   - Auto industry adjustment costs
   - Trade diversion effects
   - Labor standard improvements in Mexico (real vs. paper)

## BOTTOM LINE ASSESSMENT

**A deal will form** because the alternatives are worse for all parties. The **decisive variables** are:

1. **Chapter 19**: U.S. must retreat (Canada will not)
2. **Dairy**: Canada must concede meaningfully but limitedly (U.S. needs political win)
3. **Labor enforcement**: Must be credible enough for AFL-CIO (determines Democratic votes)

The **highest risk** is not initial signature but **U.S. Congressional ratification** in a divided government. Expect a **two-stage process**: trilateral signature (Fall 2018) followed by Democratic-demanded modifications (2019) before final passage.

**Confidence levels:**
- Trilateral deal by September 30, 2018: 65%
- Deal reached by end October 2018: 85%
- U.S. ratification without modification: 15%
- U.S. ratification after 2019 amendments: 70%
- All three countries ratify by end 2020: 60%

The **key unknown**: Whether USTR/White House will accept the Congressional modifications required for passage, or whether political dynamics (2020 election) derail the process entirely.
