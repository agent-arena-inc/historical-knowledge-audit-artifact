# SENIOR TRADE-POLICY ANALYSIS
## U.S.-Mexico-Canada Trade Negotiations (27 August 2018)

---

## 1. EXPECTED DOMINANT PACKAGE

**Base Case (65% probability): Trilateral deal by early October 2018**

The U.S.-Mexico bilateral creates enormous pressure for a trilateral conclusion:

- **Auto Rules**: ~75% RVC with labor-value content/wage floor (essentially locked by U.S.-Mexico deal)
- **Labor Standards**: Enforceable obligations with some facility-level enforcement mechanism (details TBD)
- **Chapter 19**: **Survives in modified or preserved form** — this is Canada's hardest red line
- **Dairy**: Canada grants **limited additional market access** (3-4% TRQ expansion) — painful but survivable politically
- **Sunset Clause**: **Converted to periodic review mechanism** (likely 5-6 year reviews with 10-16 year base term) — allows U.S. to claim "leverage," business to claim "certainty"
- **ISDS Reform**: Curtailed investor-state provisions (both Canada and U.S. share skepticism)

**Why this configuration?**
- Mexico has already conceded on autos; cannot walk away
- Canada faces steel/aluminum tariff leverage and auto-sector integration risk
- U.S. needs Congressional ratification, requiring some labor/Canada accommodation
- All three governments face domestic political timelines (U.S. midterms, Mexican transition, Canadian 2019 election)

**Alternative Scenario (25% probability): Talks stall into 2019**
- If Canada holds absolutely firm on dairy AND Chapter 19 simultaneously
- U.S. proceeds with bilateral Mexico deal, creating messy two-agreement structure
- Resumed negotiations in 2019 under worse political conditions

**Collapse Scenario (10% probability): No deal**
- Requires Trump to actually terminate NAFTA, which faces legal/Congressional obstacles
- Economically catastrophic; politically risky even for Trump

## 2. KEY BOTTLENECKS AND RISKS

### **DECISIVE BOTTLENECKS** (deal-breakers)

#### **A. Chapter 19 Dispute Settlement**
**Status**: Direct clash between USTR objective (eliminate) and Canadian red line (preserve)

**Why Decisive**: 
- Canada has stated this is existential — protects against U.S. trade remedy abuse (lumber, etc.)
- U.S. industry actually *split*: some sectors want it gone, others value predictability
- No middle ground is obvious (you can't have "half a dispute panel")

**Resolution Path**: 
- U.S. accepts Chapter 19 preservation in exchange for:
  - Dairy concessions from Canada
  - Face-saving "improvements" to panel procedures
  - Wins on sunset/review mechanism
- **Risk**: USTR hardliners (Lighthizer, Navarro wing) may refuse, but **business pressure will be intense** — auto/manufacturing CEOs will lobby hard for closure

#### **B. Canadian Dairy Access**
**Status**: U.S. demands meaningful market opening; Canada treats supply management as sacred

**Why Decisive**:
- **U.S. domestic politics**: Wisconsin, New York dairy states are Trump-won swing states; must show wins
- **Canadian domestic politics**: Quebec and rural Ontario dairy farmers are Liberal base; Trudeau government is minority-vulnerable
- Both sides have genuine political pain

**Resolution Path**:
- Canada grants **3.5-4% additional market access** via TRQ
- Structured as "ultra-filtered milk" or specific product categories to minimize political visibility
- U.S. declares victory; Canada claims system preserved
- **Risk**: Canadian government falls if concession seen as too large (unlikely but possible)

#### **C. Sunset Clause vs. Investment Certainty**
**Status**: U.S. wants 5-year hard sunset; business/Mexico/Canada demand permanence

**Why Decisive**:
- Auto investment cycles are 7-10 years; hard sunset kills capital deployment
- But pure permanence removes U.S. leverage (USTR's core concern)

**Resolution Path**:
- **16-year term with 6-year reviews** (or similar: 12-year with 4-year reviews)
- At each review, parties can extend or propose amendments
- No automatic termination, but structured re-evaluation
- **This is the natural compromise** — allows U.S. to claim oversight, business to plan
- **Risk**: U.S. hardliners reject as insufficient; more likely they accept as "win"

---

### **SECONDARY BOTTLENECKS** (difficult but solvable)

#### **D. Labor Enforcement Mechanism**
**Status**: Need credible enforcement without appearing to infringe Mexican sovereignty

**Resolution Path**:
- **Facility-specific complaint mechanism** for labor violations
- Independent Mexican labor tribunals (satisfies sovereignty)
- But with trade penalties available (satisfies U.S. labor/Democrats)
- Mexico already signaling willingness given AMLO's labor-reform agenda

**Risk Level**: Medium — mechanisms are negotiable; political will exists

#### **E. Auto RVC Implementation Details**
**Status**: 75% RVC + wage floor agreed in principle; phase-in and calculation details remain

**Resolution Path**:
- 3-5 year phase-in for existing models
- Alternative staging vehicles (trucks vs. passenger cars)
- **Industry will lobby hard for maximum flexibility**

**Risk Level**: Low-Medium — technical, not political; industry accepts direction

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS, RED LINES, AND LIKELY CONCESSIONS

### **USTR (Lighthizer)**

**Objectives**:
1. Eliminate Chapter 19 ✗ (will fail)
2. Higher auto RVC ✓ (achieved at 75%)
3. Dairy market access ✓ (will get limited win)
4. Sunset clause → (will get review mechanism)
5. Enforceable labor ✓ (achievable)

**Red Lines**:
- Must show "wins" vs. old NAFTA for Trump
- Cannot accept pure status quo

**Likely Concessions**:
- Accepts Chapter 19 survival (spun as "improved" panels)
- Accepts review mechanism instead of hard sunset
- Accepts smaller dairy opening than demanded

**Leverage**:
- Steel/aluminum tariffs (Section 232) still in place
- Threat of bilateral Mexico-only deal
- Congressional fast-track deadline pressure

---

### **CANADA (Freeland Government)**

**Objectives**:
1. Preserve Chapter 19 ✓ (likely succeeds)
2. Protect dairy supply management ~ (will give limited ground)
3. Maintain regulatory sovereignty ✓
4. Modernize labor/environment ✓

**Red Lines**:
- **Chapter 19 is absolute** — will walk away without it
- Dairy concessions cannot exceed ~5% (political survival threshold)

**Likely Concessions**:
- 3-4% dairy market access (painful but survivable)
- Accepts higher auto RVC (hurts but manageable)
- Accepts periodic review mechanism (better than sunset)

**Leverage**:
- Can credibly threaten to walk (unlike Mexico, which already signed)
- U.S. needs trilateral deal for Congressional ratification
- Time pressure helps Canada (U.S. midterms approaching)

**Vulnerability**:
- Steel/aluminum tariffs hurting industry
- Auto sector integrated; disruption risk high

---

### **MEXICO (Peña Nieto / Incoming AMLO)**

**Position**:

**Red Lines**:
- Cannot reopen auto terms already agreed

**Likely Concessions**:

**Leverage**:
- Minimal — already committed

### **U.S. CONGRESS / LABOR (AFL-CIO, UAW)**

**Objectives**:
1. **Real labor enforcement** (not just text)
2. Prevent further offshoring
3. Higher wages in Mexico

**Red Lines**:
- Will not support deal without credible labor enforcement
- Democratic votes needed in House (even with Republican control, need bipartisan margin)

**Position**:

- Can be won over with:
  - Facility-specific enforcement mechanism
  - Elimination of investor-state (ISDS) or major curtailment
  - Higher auto content rules (already achieved)

**Ratification Risk**: 
- **This is the decisive U.S. domestic constituency**
- Without labor support, House passage is uncertain
- Likely demand "improvements" to whatever is signed (see Section 4)

---

### **AUTO INDUSTRY (GM, Ford, Toyota, suppliers)**

**Objectives**:

  - Long phase-in (5+ years)
  - Flexible calculation methods
  - Review mechanism instead of sunset

**Leverage**:
- Major employers in swing states
- Credible threat to shift investment to Asia if rules unworkable

### **U.S. DAIRY / AGRICULTURE**

### **NORTH AMERICAN MANUFACTURERS (broader)**

## 4. DOMESTIC RATIFICATION RISKS

### **U.S. RATIFICATION: HIGH RISK**

#### **Political Environment (Late 2018/Early 2019)**:
- Republican Congress (lame duck) OR new Democratic House (post-midterms)
- Democrats will control process if they win House
- Labor unions are **swing constituency**

#### **Key Ratification Bottlenecks**:

**A. Labor Enforcement Credibility** (DECISIVE)
- **Problem**: AFL-CIO, UAW will scrutinize enforcement mechanism
- If they see "NAFTA 2.0" with weak enforcement, they oppose
- **Likely outcome**: Democrats demand **additional protocols** or **side letters**
  - Strengthening rapid-response mechanism
  - Guaranteeing independent Mexican labor tribunals
  - Appropriations for monitoring/enforcement

**Expected Process**:
1. Initial text signed (Fall 2018)
2. Democrats win House (Nov 2018) — *probable*
3. New Congress demands modifications (early 2019)
4. **Protocol of Amendment negotiated** (Spring/Summer 2019)
5. Ratification vote (late 2019/early 2020)

**Probability of passage**: 60-65% *if* labor enforcement strengthened; 30% without

---

**B. Pharmaceutical/Biologics Provisions**
- **Problem**: If USTR includes extended data exclusivity for biologics (10 years), Democrats oppose as drug-price protection
- **Solution**: Likely stripped or reduced in ratification process

---

**C. Sunset/Review Mechanism**
- **Problem**: If final text has hard sunset, business lobbies against
- **Solution**: Review mechanism satisfies both business and enough Republicans

---

**D. Democratic vs. Republican Split**:
- **Traditional Republicans**: Free-trade oriented; support modernized NAFTA
- **Trump Republicans**: Populist; want "tough" deal but will follow Trump
- **Democrats**: Split between:
  - Pro-labor progressives (need enforcement)
  - Moderate/business Dems (support trade)
- **Winning coalition**: Trump + moderate Dems + business Republicans = narrow majority
  - **Requires labor buy-in**

---

### **Highest Risk**:
**U.S. Congressional ratification** — requires Democratic buy-in, which requires labor support, which requires enforcement mechanism improvements *after* initial signature. This creates 12-18 month ratification timeline with meaningful failure risk (35-40%).
