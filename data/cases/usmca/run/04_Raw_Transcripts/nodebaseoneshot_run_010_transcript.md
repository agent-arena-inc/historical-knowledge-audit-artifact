# SENIOR TRADE-POLICY ANALYSIS
**Date: 27 August 2018**  
**Subject: Post-U.S.-Mexico Bilateral Agreement – Trilateral Package Formation & Ratification Risk**

---

## 1. EXPECTED DOMINANT PACKAGE (Probability Assessment)

**Base Case (65% probability): Trilateral deal closes within 4-6 weeks**

The U.S.-Mexico bilateral creates enormous pressure for a trilateral conclusion:

- **Structural forcing mechanism**: Trump can theoretically proceed with bilateral ratification under TPA, threatening Canada with exclusion from its largest export market ($350B+ annually, 75% of Canadian exports). This is economically catastrophic leverage.

- **Political timeline pressure**: 
  - U.S. midterm elections (November 2018) create urgency for Trump to claim a "win"
  - Mexican President-elect AMLO takes office December 1; current government wants to lock the deal
  - Trump has publicly set end-of-September deadline

**Expected package contours**:
- Canada accepts the U.S.-Mexico auto framework (75% RVC, labor-value content) with minor sectoral carve-outs
- **Partial dairy concession**: Canada opens 3-4% additional market access (enough for U.S. to declare victory, small enough for Canadian political survival)
- **Chapter 19 compromise**: Retained in modified form or with procedural constraints (both sides claim partial victory)
- **Sunset mechanism compromise**: Multi-year review process instead of hard termination (16-18 year term with periodic reviews)

**Alternative Scenario (25% probability): Talks stall into 2019**

If Canada holds firm on all red lines simultaneously, or if U.S. demands become non-negotiable on Chapter 19 elimination:
- Negotiation extends past midterms
- Democrat House takeover (if it occurs) reshapes dynamics
- Mexico may be forced to implement bilaterally or freeze

**Low probability (10%): No deal / NAFTA termination**

Trump follows through on withdrawal threats; economically irrational but politically possible.

## 2. KEY BOTTLENECKS & DOWNSTREAM RISKS

### TIER 1 DECISIVE BOTTLENECKS (Deal-Breakers)

#### A. **Chapter 19 Dispute Settlement** ⚠️ CRITICAL
- **U.S. position**: USTR mandate explicitly requires elimination
- **Canadian position**: Existential red line; protects against U.S. AD/CVD abuse (softwood lumber history)
- **Why decisive**: No obvious middle ground exists between "eliminate" and "preserve"
- **Likely resolution path**: 
  - Procedural modifications (stricter timelines, narrower scope)
  - Elevated political review mechanism as alternative
  - U.S. declares "reform" while Canada keeps core function
- **Downstream risk**: Whatever compromise emerges will face challenges:
  - U.S. Congress Republicans may view retention as capitulation
  - Canadian opposition will attack any weakening

#### B. **Dairy Market Access** ⚠️ CRITICAL
- **Why decisive**: Trump has personally fixated on this; Wisconsin/dairy-state politics matter for GOP
- **Canadian domestic politics**: Supply management has Quebec farmer constituency; Trudeau minority position vulnerable
- **Likely resolution**: 
  - 3.25-3.9% market opening (precedent: TPP gave ~3.25%)
  - Structured as TRQ with growth trajectory
  - Canada frames as "TPP-equivalent" concession
- **Downstream risk**: 
  - Canadian ratification faces dairy-region MP revolt
  - U.S. may still claim insufficient access post-deal

#### C. **Sunset/Term Certainty** ⚠️ HIGH IMPACT
- **Business community unity**: Auto, manufacturing universally oppose hard sunset (kills 5-7 year investment cycles)
- **U.S. leverage motive**: Wants periodic renegotiation hammer
- **Likely resolution**: 
  - 16-year initial term
  - 6-year review with joint extension option
  - Allows U.S. to claim "oversight" while providing investment horizon
- **Downstream risk**: 
  - Review mechanism itself creates periodic uncertainty
  - 2024 review coincides with potential political transition

### TIER 2 IMPORTANT BUT NEGOTIABLE

#### D. **Auto Rules Compliance Costs**
- **Current state**: 75% RVC + labor-value content already agreed U.S.-Mexico
- **Canada's challenge**: Accept framework negotiated without Canadian input
- **Resolution**: Minor sectoral adjustments, extended phase-in (4-5 years)
- **Downstream risk**: 
  - Compliance cost studies post-signature may show 2-4% vehicle price increases
  - Auto industry grudging acceptance, not enthusiasm

#### E. **Labor Enforcement Mechanism**
- **Mexican sensitivity**: Sovereignty concerns over facility-level enforcement
- **U.S./Canada labor requirement**: Must have "teeth" for ratification support
- **Resolution path**: Facility-specific complaint mechanism with escalation
- **Downstream risk**: Implementation disputes 2020+; first test cases will be politically charged

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS, CONCESSIONS & RED LINES

### USTR (Robert Lighthizer)

**Objectives hierarchy**:
1. ✅ **Already secured**: Higher auto RVC, labor-value content (Mexico bilateral)
2. 🎯 **Must achieve for political credibility**: 
   - Dairy market access (Trump's personal priority)
   - Some form of Chapter 19 "reform" to claim objective met
3. 🎯 **Prefer but tradeable**: 
   - Complete Chapter 19 elimination (will accept compromise)
   - Hard sunset clause (will accept review mechanism)

**Red lines**: Cannot return with zero dairy access and unchanged Chapter 19

**Likely concessions**: 
- Chapter 19 retention with modifications
- Sunset becomes review mechanism
- Agricultural access narrower than initial ask

---

### CANADA (Chrystia Freeland / Trudeau Government)

**Red lines** (in order of firmness):
1. **Chapter 19 core function preservation** – existential for anti-dumping defense
2. **Supply management system survival** – can give access but not dismantle system
3. **Cultural exemptions** – secondary but symbolically important

**Forced concessions** (economic gun to head):
- Accept U.S.-Mexico auto framework (75% RVC, wage floors)
- Dairy market opening 3-4% (painful but survivable)
- Sunset compromise (review mechanism)

**Negotiating strategy**: 
- Maximize delay to demonstrate "standing up" domestically
- Frame any dairy concession as equivalent to TPP (already-accepted baseline)
- Trade cultural/digital commitments for Chapter 19 retention

**Domestic political risk**: 
- Dairy-region Liberal MPs may revolt
- Conservative opposition will attack either "capitulation" or "risking jobs"
- Must show toughness without killing deal

---

**Constraints**:

- AMLO team watching but not controlling

**Red line**: Cannot appear to reopen auto terms now locked with U.S.

**Role in Canada endgame**: Cheerleader for closure; no substantive leverage

## 4. DOWNSTREAM RATIFICATION RISKS

### TIMING SCENARIOS & IMPLICATIONS

**Scenario A: Deal signed before November 2018 midterms**
- Republican Congress ratifies quickly (Q4 2018/Q1 2019)
- Labor opposition muted by timeline pressure
- **Risk level**: MODERATE
- **Key variable**: Chapter 19 compromise acceptable to GOP?

**Scenario B: Deal signed after midterms, Democrat House (likely)**
- Ratification delayed into 2019
- Democrats demand labor enforcement strengthening
- Potential for reopening/side letters
- **Risk level**: HIGH
- **Key variables**: 
  - Pelosi's willingness to give Trump trade win
  - AFL-CIO position on enforcement
  - 2020 presidential race dynamics (Democrats may withhold to deny Trump achievement)

### SPECIFIC RATIFICATION VULNERABILITIES

#### 1. **Labor Enforcement Gap** 🔴 HIGH RISK
- **Problem**: Current package has framework but not detailed facility-level enforcement
- **Who objects**: AFL-CIO, Democrats, UAW
- **Resolution requirement**: 
  - Detailed rapid-response mechanism (possibly via side letter/protocol)
  - Mexican legislative follow-through on labor reform
  - Demonstrated enforcement cases before ratification vote

**Probability of requiring substantive fix**: 75%

#### 2. **Chapter 19 Compromise Fragility** 🟡 MEDIUM RISK
- **Problem**: Whatever compromise emerges will disappoint purists on both sides
- **U.S. vulnerability**: GOP free-traders + anti-dumping hawks may unite in opposition if seen as weak
- **Canadian vulnerability**: Opposition attacks as "selling out" sovereignty protection

**Probability of becoming flashpoint**: 45%

#### 3. **Auto Industry Compliance Cost Backlash** 🟡 MEDIUM RISK
- **Problem**: Post-signature analysis shows 2-4% price increases, job losses from plant closures
- **Timing**: Studies emerge during ratification debate (Q1-Q2 2019)
- **Who objects**: Industry won't actively oppose but won't defend; consumer groups, free-trade economists amplify

**Probability of derailing ratification**: 25% (slows but doesn't kill)

#### 4. **Sunset/Review Mechanism Business Uncertainty** 🟡 MEDIUM RISK
- **Problem**: 6-year review creates perceived investment risk
- **Who objects**: Chambers of Commerce, manufacturers, auto suppliers
- **Mitigation**: Long initial term (16 years) + joint extension option reduces concern

**Probability of becoming major issue**: 35%

#### 5. **Canadian Ratification Failure** 🟢 LOW-MODERATE RISK
- **Problem**: Trudeau minority government loses dairy-region MPs; opposition unites against
- **Mitigating factors**: 
  - Canadian economic dependence makes rejection near-suicidal
  - Conservative opposition historically pro-trade
  - Framing as "best available" under U.S. pressure

**Probability**: 20% (but would be catastrophic)
