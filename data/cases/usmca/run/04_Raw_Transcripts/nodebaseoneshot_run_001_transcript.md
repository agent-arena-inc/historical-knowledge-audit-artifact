# SENIOR TRADE-POLICY ANALYSIS
**Date: 27 August 2018**  
**Subject: Post-U.S.-Mexico Bilateral Agreement — Path to Trilateral Package**

---

## 1. DOMINANT PACKAGE EXPECTATION

**Base Case (65% probability): Trilateral agreement reached within 4-6 weeks**

Canada will join a modified trilateral package under intense time pressure, but only after extracting critical concessions on Chapter 19 and limiting dairy exposure. The dominant package will likely feature:

- **Auto RoO**: ~75% RVC with labor-value content requirements (per U.S.-Mexico bilateral template)
- **Labor**: Enforceable obligations with some facility-level enforcement mechanism (Mexico accepts in principle; details TBD)
- **Chapter 19**: **Retained** (Canada's hard red line; U.S. forced to concede)
- **Dairy**: Canada grants **limited new market access** (~3-4% TRQ increase) — painful but survivable politically
- **Sunset clause**: Replaced by a **review mechanism** (16-20 year term with periodic reviews) to address investment certainty concerns

**Alternative Scenario (25% probability): Talks stall into Q4 2018**

If U.S. refuses to budge on Chapter 19 or demands >5% dairy access, Canada walks. U.S.-Mexico bilateral proceeds as standalone, creating legal/political chaos and likely Congressional rejection.

**Low Probability (10%): U.S.-Mexico bilateral only**

Trump proceeds without Canada; faces certain Congressional defeat and auto industry revolt.

## 2. KEY NEGOTIATION BOTTLENECKS & DOWNSTREAM RISKS

### **DECISIVE BOTTLENECKS** (Deal-Breakers)

#### **A. Chapter 19 Binational Panels** ⚠️ CRITICAL
- **Conflict**: U.S. objective explicitly states "eliminate Chapter 19"; Canada treats this as existential red line
- **Why decisive**: Canada has stated publicly it will not sign without this; no face-saving middle ground exists (it's binary: in or out)
- **Likely resolution**: **U.S. concedes; Chapter 19 survives**
  - Rationale: U.S. needs trilateral deal for Congressional passage; Mexico already locked in; Trump needs "win" before midterms
  - USTR can spin as "preserved trade remedy enforcement" while getting wins elsewhere
- **Downstream risk**: U.S. softwood lumber/trade remedy interests furious; potential ratification opposition from protectionist Republicans

#### **B. Dairy Market Access** ⚠️ CRITICAL
- **Conflict**: U.S. dairy demands meaningful access vs. Canadian supply management (political third rail in Quebec/Ontario)
- **Why decisive**: Trump has personally attacked Canadian dairy; U.S. ag sector expects concrete gains; but Canadian government cannot survive giving away >5% without political compensation
- **Likely resolution**: **Canada grants 3.5-4.5% additional TRQ** (Class 6/7 ultra-filtered milk addressed)
  - Structured as "growth" access to minimize domestic blowback
  - Canada extracts Chapter 19 retention as political cover
- **Downstream risk**: 
  - **Canadian ratification**: Quebec dairy lobby mobilizes against Trudeau
  - **U.S. ratification**: If access seen as insufficient, Upper Midwest senators (WI, MN) could defect

#### **C. Sunset/Termination Clause** ⚠️ CRITICAL
- **Conflict**: U.S. wants automatic termination (5-year sunset) vs. Canada/Mexico/business demand for investment certainty
- **Why decisive**: Auto industry will actively oppose any hard sunset; 5-10 year vehicle development cycles require stable rules
- **Likely resolution**: **16-18 year term with 6-year review mechanism** (no automatic termination)
  - Reviews allow U.S. to claim "ongoing leverage"
  - Long term satisfies investment planning
- **Downstream risk**: 
  - **Ratification**: Trump base may see long term as "permanent NAFTA"
  - **Implementation**: First review (2024-25) becomes high-stakes political fight

### **SECONDARY BOTTLENECKS** (Manageable)

#### **D. Auto Rules of Origin — Implementation Details**
- **Issue**: 75% RVC + labor-value content agreed in principle (U.S.-Mexico), but phase-in timeline, calculation methodology, and compliance verification unresolved
- **Stakeholder tension**: Auto manufacturers want maximum flexibility/transition time vs. USTR/labor want strict enforcement
- **Likely resolution**: 3-5 year phase-in with alternative staging for different vehicle classes
- **Downstream risk**: Compliance costs higher than modeled; some production shifts to Asia (perverse outcome)

#### **E. Labor Enforcement Mechanism Design**
- **Issue**: Mexico agreed to labor reform in principle, but enforcement modality unclear
- **Tension**: U.S. labor demands facility-specific enforcement vs. Mexico sovereignty concerns
- **Likely resolution**: Hybrid state-to-state + facility-specific rapid complaints for "pattern" violations
- **Downstream risk**: If mechanism too weak, AFL-CIO opposes ratification; if too strong, Mexican Senate balks

## 3. STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS, CONCESSIONS & RED LINES

### **USTR (Robert Lighthizer)**
- **Objectives**: Higher RoO, enforceable labor, dairy access, eliminate Chapter 19, sunset clause, declare "win"
- **Red Lines**: Must have enforceable labor (credibility with Congress); must show dairy gains (Trump promise)
- **Likely Concessions**: 
  - ✓ Chapter 19 survives (spun as preserving trade remedy rights)
  - ✓ Sunset becomes review mechanism (spun as "regular renegotiation opportunity")
- **Leverage**: Mexico already locked in; Canada faces September 30 Congressional notification deadline pressure

### **Canada (Chrystia Freeland)**
- **Objectives**: Preserve Chapter 19, protect supply management, modernize labor/environment, maintain regulatory sovereignty
- **Red Lines**: 
  - **ABSOLUTE**: Chapter 19 must survive
  - **CRITICAL**: Dairy access <5% (political survival threshold)
- **Likely Concessions**:
  - ✓ 3.5-4.5% dairy TRQ (painful but survivable with Chapter 19 win)
  - ✓ Accept U.S.-Mexico auto RoO framework (limited input)
- **Leverage**: U.S. needs trilateral for Congress; Canadian market matters for U.S. exporters; can credibly walk away

### **Mexico (Ildefonso Guajardo / incoming López Obrador team)**
- **Objectives**: Lock in August bilateral; avoid being squeezed in U.S.-Canada fight; secure transition certainty for AMLO government (Dec 1 inauguration)
- **Red Lines**: Bilateral gains cannot be reopened; labor reform acceptable if sovereignty-respecting
- **Likely Concessions**: Already made (75% RoO, labor-value content, labor reform commitments)
- **Leverage**: Minimal (already committed); wants deal before government transition

### **U.S. Congress / Labor (AFL-CIO, UAW)**
- **Objectives**: Enforceable labor standards (especially Mexico wage/union reform); auto jobs protection; Chapter 19 less critical for labor
- **Red Lines**: Labor enforcement must be credible and facility-specific
- **Likely Position**: Support IF:
  - Strong labor enforcement mechanism included
  - Auto RoO seen as job-protective (75% + wage floor acceptable)
  - Mexico demonstrates legislative follow-through on labor reform
- **Ratification Risk**: **MODERATE-HIGH** — Democrats will demand proof of enforcement; timeline for Mexican labor law passage critical

### **Auto Industry (Detroit 3, suppliers, Mexico/Canada plants)**
- **Objectives**: Minimize disruption; maximize phase-in time; preserve integrated supply chains; eliminate sunset uncertainty
- **Red Lines**: No hard sunset; reasonable compliance timeline
- **Likely Position**: **Reluctant acceptance** IF:
  - 4-5 year phase-in for RoO
  - No automatic termination (review mechanism acceptable)
  - Compliance verification not punitive
- **Ratification Risk**: **MODERATE** — Will not actively oppose if phase-in adequate, but won't champion; focus on implementation regulations

### **U.S. Dairy/Agriculture**
- **Objectives**: Meaningful Canadian market access (Class 6/7 ultra-filtered milk; broader TRQ)
- **Red Lines**: Must show concrete gains vs. status quo
- **Likely Position**: Support IF Canada grants 3-4%+ access
- **Ratification Risk**: **LOW-MODERATE** — Upper Midwest senators critical; if access <3%, could sink deal in Senate

### **North American Manufacturers (supply-chain integrated)**
- **Objectives**: Preserve tariff-free access; regulatory alignment; no sunset
- **Red Lines**: Investment certainty (no 5-year cliff)
- **Likely Position**: Support any trilateral deal without hard sunset
- **Ratification Risk**: **LOW** — Business community will lobby for passage

**Risk Level: HIGH (50% passage probability under current conditions)**

**Vulnerabilities:**

- **Republican free-trade opposition**: Protectionist wing (esp. if Chapter 19 survives) may defect
- **Timing**: 2019 vote faces divided Congress (Democrats likely take House in Nov 2018 midterms)

**Passage Factors:**
- ✓ TPA fast-track limits amendments (up-or-down vote)
- ✓ Business community unified support (if sunset resolved)
- ✓ Agricultural sector support (if dairy gains adequate)
- ✓ Trump will make passage a priority (political win needed)
- ✗ Pelosi-led House Democrats will demand labor enforcement proof
- ✗ Progressive Democrats (Warren, Sanders wing) likely oppose
- ✗ Senate arithmetic: need ~10-15 Democrats; labor endorsement critical

**DECISIVE FACTORS for U.S. Ratification:**

1. **Mexican Labor Reform Legislative Progress** ⚠️ CRITICAL
   - Congress will demand Mexico pass constitutional labor law reforms BEFORE U.S. ratification vote
   - Timeline: Mexico needs to pass laws in early 2019 for U.S. vote by mid-2019
   - **Risk**: AMLO government slow-walks reforms; U.S. vote delayed into 2020 election year (toxic politics)

2. **AFL-CIO/Labor Endorsement** ⚠️ CRITICAL
   - Democratic leadership will not move without labor buy-in
   - Labor demands: (a) credible facility-specific enforcement, (b) proof of Mexican implementation, (c) removal of ISDS investor protections
   - **Risk**: If enforcement mechanism seen as weak, labor opposes → Democratic "no" votes → deal dies

3. **Auto Industry Neutrality vs. Opposition**
   - If phase-in <3 years or compliance costs exceed $1000/vehicle, industry actively opposes
   - Active opposition = Republican defections in MI, OH, TX → deal in jeopardy
   - **Risk**: USTR underestimates compliance costs; industry turns against deal in 2019

4. **Dairy Sector Satisfaction**
   - Need WI, MN, PA senators (both parties)
   - If Canada access <3%, dairy lobby opposes → Upper Midwest votes at risk
   - **Risk**: Canada grants only token access; U.S. dairy mobilizes against

5. **Political Calendar**
   - **Optimal window**: Jan-June 2019 (before 2020 primary season)
   - **Risk**: Delayed signing → vote slips to late 2019 or 2020 → presidential politics poison well

### **RATIFICATION SEQUENCING RISK** ⚠️

**Likely Resolution**:

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The deal is makeable but fragile.** The U.S.-Mexico bilateral creates momentum, but Canada holds veto power via Chapter 19 and dairy. The dominant package will reflect mutual concessions on red-line issues:

- **U.S. wins**: Higher auto RoO, enforceable labor, some dairy access, review mechanism
- **Canada wins**: Chapter 19 survival, limited dairy damage
- **Mexico wins**: Deal certainty before AMLO transition

**The decisive ratification risk is U.S. Congressional passage**, which hinges entirely on:
1. Mexican labor reform implementation (proof required)
2. AFL-CIO endorsement (enforcement credibility)
3. Auto industry non-opposition (phase-in adequacy)
